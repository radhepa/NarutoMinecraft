Tags: **[S]** = stated by a cited source. **[M]** = from series memory, not confirmed by a source reachable here. Check [M] items against screenshots before treating them as canon.

## A. Canon Konoha (Part 1, before the Pain attack)

### Size and shape
- **Size [S]:** Baidu Baike gives the area as about 400,000 m² (Baidu 1), which is about 632 × 632 m. **A 640-block plan is canon scale.**
- **Shape [S]:** Roughly fan-shaped (Baidu 1). Narutopedia says the village is "built around the Hokage Rock in a fan-like formation" (NP-Konoha, from a search snippet). Practical reading: the Rock is the hinge of the fan, the village spreads out downhill from its base, and the wall closes the arc.
- **Setting [S]:** Deep in a forest, at the foot of a mountain that carries the Hokage faces (NP-Konoha, NaruSaku wiki). Layered mountains rise behind the village, and rivers run through the forest like a network (Baidu 1).
- **Real-world reference [S]:** The landscape comes from Kishimoto's childhood surroundings in Okayama: a quiet village, forests and rivers. He has said Konoha's climate and topography would be close to Kyoto's. Its era is roughly present day (Wikipedia "Naruto Universe", kiwix mirror). Read this as mixed rural forest plus a modern small town, not Edo.
- **Building colour [S]:** Baidu says buildings are "mainly yellow", standing for vitality and hope (Baidu 1). This supports smooth_sandstone cream as the dominant wall.

### Hokage Rock
- **The cliff [S]:** The faces are carved into the cliff of the highest peak behind the village. Visitors see it first on entering. Its top looks over the whole village (Baidu 1; NP-Hokage Rock).
- **Faces in Part 1 [S]:** Four faces: First to Fourth. The Fifth (Tsunade) was added during the timeskip, so it is post-Part 1 (Wikipedia 2007 "Land of Fire" mirror).
- **Inside the Rock [S]:** A hidden chamber under the Rock serves as the evacuation shelter for non-combatants, and the Rock was also used for archives (Baidu 1; NP-Hokage Rock).
- **Above the faces [S]:** Forest in Part 1. Only after the Fourth War does the village expand onto the plateau above the monument, "with the village's new skyscrapers" (NP-Hokage Rock, snippet). **Keep the top as forest. Put no buildings above the faces.**
- **Viewpoint [M]:** A railed lookout and stairs on top, directly above the faces. Naruto and the Third look out over the village from up there.

### Gate and wall
- **Gate count [S]:** Four entrance gates. The hiragana あん is painted on them, one character per door: あ on one, ん on the other. It reads 庵, "hermitage, retreat" (NaruSaku wiki; Wikipedia 2007 mirror). The a-un (阿吽) idea means beginning and end, as with komainu statues (Wikipedia "Komainu"; X post by @rei5131). The later name "A-Un Gate" comes from Boruto episode 176 (Geekiary review).
- **Gate look [M]:** Two very tall green doors with large red あ / ん. They are held in a heavy timber post-and-lintel frame with a tiled cap, and a small guard booth stands just inside, with two chūnin on desk duty.
- **Wall [S]:** The village is "surrounded on all sides by enormous walls", but this phrasing comes from fan wikis (Naruto/One Piece/Fairy Tail fan wiki). NaruSaku wiki says torii are built around the entire wall. The only height figure found is a VS Battles pixel estimate of about 50 m, which is contested and could not be fetched.
- **Wall at game scale [M]:** A plain pale/beige wall with a darker cap. Use 15–25 blocks so the 70-block Rock still dominates.
- **Barrier [S]:** A spherical sensor barrier covers the village, including underground. It is invisible except to the Rinnegan, and a surveillance unit watches it (Wikipedia "Naruto Universe"). No block form needed.
- **Gate position [M]:** The main gate sits on the far side of the fan, roughly opposite the Rock, so whoever enters walks toward the faces (Baidu: "the first thing visitors see"). No source gives a compass bearing. One low-quality page says the Rock overlooks the village "from the north". Suggested layout: Rock at the north, main gate at the south, main street running north from the gate to the Academy and Residence at the foot of the Rock.

### Main road, Residence and Academy
- **Main road [M]:** A wide, earthen/packed street from the gate to the administrative core at the base of the Rock. Shops on both sides carry vertical banners and noren.
- **Hokage Residence [S]:** Snippet facts:
  - A large circular building, one of the largest in Konoha.
  - Near both the Academy and the Rock.
  - "the Fire kanji painted on the roof".
- **Residence vs office [S]:** Narutopedia notes that official sources treat the Residence and the Academy (whose admin wing holds the Hokage's office) as separate places (NP-Hokage Residence).
- **Residence look [M]:** A red/orange drum-shaped tower, several storeys tall, with a shallow conical/flat roof and a white 火 disc. It sits beside or in front of the Academy, under the Rock.
- **Academy [S]:** A large building "directly at the base of the Hokage Mountain". Its roof, under the Rock, is used for lectures (NP, snippet). A fan blog mentions a tree in front with a swing and a big 火 sign (Borutobucks blog; low reliability).
- **Academy look [M]:** A long 3-storey building with pale walls, a green or terracotta roof edge and a central entrance. Classes sit at bench rows. A practice yard and a big tree with a rope swing sit at the front side.

### Hospital
- **Location [S]:** No source found for its location.
- **Rooftop [S]:** Canon has water tanks on the roof. In episode 107 the Naruto vs Sasuke fight takes place on the hospital roof, and Kakashi deflects the Rasengan and Chidori into two water tanks (FandomWire; Bubbleblabber; thetvdb).
- **Look [M]:** A tall rectangular block, among the tallest buildings in Part 1 (roughly 5–7 floors). Pale walls with window bands, a flat roof with a parapet and two cylindrical tanks on legs. It stands mid-village, not at the edge.

### Ichiraku
- **Form [S]:** A yatai-style counter stall run by Teuchi and Ayame (Nijigen no Mori replica; SoraNews24).
- **Look [M]:** A counter with about 6 stools under a short roof. Noren hangs over the front edge, the cook works behind the counter, and it opens straight onto a busy street in the central shopping district. No source gives its exact block.

### Uchiha district
- **Location [S]:** After the Nine-Tails attack the clan was moved to a compound on the village outskirts, "necessary for reconstruction". The Military Police HQ was in a remote part of the village (NP-Uchiha Clan, snippet). Baidu says the Police HQ was built with the prison.
- **Police crest [S]:** The Uchiha crest inside a shuriken (NP-Uchiha Clan, snippet).
- **Look [M]:** A walled district with an entrance arch. White uchiwa fan crests sit on walls and lanterns. The buildings are traditional 1–2 storey wood-and-plaster houses with grey kawara roofs. The police HQ is a large building with the crest. Naka Shrine stands nearby, with its secret meeting hall under the 7th tatami mat.
- **Naka River [S]:** Flows through Konoha on the former Uchiha lands and passes through a deep ravine at one point (NP-Naka River, snippet).
- **Lake [M]:** A wooden jetty on a lake/pond near the district, where young Sasuke practised the fireball.

### Hyūga and Nara
- **Hyūga [S]:** A large walled estate. The main-branch residence and a larger branch-family residence sit inside the same compound (fan-wiki snippets).
- **Hyūga look [M]:** Formal wooden architecture, an engawa round a gravel and sand training courtyard, and dark grey roofs.
- **Nara [S]:** No reliable source reached.
- **Nara [M]:** The Nara clan keeps a fenced forest with deer, whose antlers go into medicine. It is shown clearly in Shippūden but exists before. Put it on the village edge against the wall.

### Training grounds
- **Training Ground 3 [S]:** Where Team 7 did the bell test (NP-Third Training Ground, snippet).
- **Training Ground 3 look [M]:** A grass clearing with three wooden posts in a row and a memorial stone (cenotaph). Forest and a small river border it.
- **Training Ground 44 / Forest of Death [S]:**
  - A metal fence with 44 equally spaced gates.
  - Inside: a river, forest and a central tower with four entrances and an arena inside.
  - "radius of about ten kilometres" (Wikipedia 2007 mirror; NP-Forest of Death, snippet).
  - Its position relative to the village is unconfirmed; "south of the village" comes only from a fan RPG page.
- **Training Ground 44 at game scale:** At 10 km it cannot fit inside 640 blocks. Show the fence line and one numbered gate at the edge, and abstract the forest beyond it.

### Skyline [M] (Part 1 anime backgrounds)
- **Massing:** Dense 2–5 storey buildings at a small-town scale. Walls are cream, beige, white and pale pink/peach.
- **Roofs:** Many are flat with a parapet. Others are low-pitched in red-orange, green/teal or grey tile, and kawara ridges are common.
- **Rooftop clutter:** Cylindrical water tanks on legs (canon example: the hospital), exterior pipes and vents.
- **Signs and wires:** Vertical kanji shop signs. Wooden utility poles with sagging wires along streets.
- **Shapes:** Occasional drum or cylinder buildings and round towers. Big broadleaf trees grow between buildings.
- **Ground:** Gently rolling, with stairs and stepped lanes between levels.
- The water-tower motif matches real Japanese housing estates (tkgje.jp "kyuusuitou"; no Naruto link).

### Naruto's apartment
- **Valuation [S]:** Movoto valued it at $100k using about 400 sq ft (~37 m²) at $250/sq ft (Gizmodo; ANN; SoraNews24). One write-up calls it a one-bedroom on the top floor with a village view (Gizmodo snippet).
- **Interior [M]:** One main room with the bed under a window and potted plants on the sill. A small kitchen corner with a table, cup ramen and milk in the fridge, plus a cluttered floor and a small bathroom.
- **Building [M]:** A modest 2–3 storey block with an outside stair and flat roof. Naruto exits by jumping across rooftops.

### Era traps to avoid
- Post-Pain rebuild (Shippūden ep. 167+) and Boruto Konoha: no skyscrapers, video screens, trains, highways, or buildings on top of the Rock (NP-Hokage Rock snippet on the plateau expansion).
- No Fifth face in Part 1; at most a timeskip toggle.
- No Pain crater.

### Layout references (maps)
- No official databook map with district positions was found. The only labelled layouts are fan text guides, such as a "Partisan" location guide on roleplayerguild (blocked here).
- **Game maps:** deav29's 5-year Minecraft Konoha took its layout from the open-world Konoha in *Naruto: Ultimate Ninja Storm* (Part 1 era) and *Naruto Shippuden: Ultimate Ninja Storm Generations / Broken Bonds* (comicbook.com). *Ultimate Ninja Storm 1* and *Rise of a Ninja* (2007) are the most useful Part 1 free-roam references. [M: Rise of a Ninja]

## B. Minecraft technique (block level)

### Existing Konoha and Japanese builds (pages mostly blocked; descriptions only)
- **deav29 Konoha (Reddit):** Five years of work. The builder calls the buildings "pretty accurate" and took the layout from the Storm and Broken Bonds game maps (comicbook.com).
- **CursedFoundry "Naruto: Hidden Leaf Village [Konoha Map]":** On CurseForge for 1.21.1, back to 1.7.10. It has streets, landmarks and the Rock, with about 54k downloads; the page states no size (CurseForge).
- **Other Konoha maps:**
  - Planet Minecraft "Konohagakure – Hidden Leaf" (403; commenters asked for the five Hokage faces).
  - BuiltByBit "Hidden Leaf Village V2", built beneath the Rock.
  - JambleStudios' whole-Naruto-world build (Sportskeeda).
- **Japanese cities:** Planet Minecraft "Tatsuyama, Empire of the Dragon Mountain" (multi-district feudal city), "Kyoto" town and "Furusato Mountain Village", plus CurseForge "Japanese City [Castle Edo]".
- **Tutorials:** Folli, "How to Build the Ultimate Japanese House + Interior" (roof section at 59:53, 1.17.1). French series "Construire un Village Japonais de A à Z" (gate, forge, bridges and roads episodes).

### Wall texture and gradients
- **Rules [S]:** Conquest Reforged gradient guide:
  - Step between neighbouring values only, and never let distant colours touch.
  - Clustered gradients form irregular clumps, never rows or columns.
  - Avoid random splatter on visible faces.
  - Darken the blocks directly under windows (water staining).
  - Put weathering where rain collects.
  - Squint to judge value.
- **Calcite vs quartz [S]:** Calcite is greyer and grainier than quartz, which makes it a good large-wall white. Diorite is speckled and suits a gradient or base (Sportskeeda; minecraft.how).
- **Cream plaster recipe:**
  - Field: about 85% smooth_sandstone.
  - Clusters: cut_sandstone and sandstone, about 15%.
  - Under sills: sandstone.
  - Lowest course: a 1-block stone footing in tuff, polished_andesite or stone_bricks.
  - Above the footing: a 1-block grime band of sandstone mixed with a little smooth_red_sandstone, only on street-facing walls.
- **White plaster recipe:**
  - Field: smooth_quartz. Use calcite for large walls, mixed 70/30 with smooth_quartz in clusters.
  - Under windows: polished_diorite.
  - Base: diorite then andesite.
- **Terracotta walls:** Terracotta field with brown_terracotta clusters low down, and light_gray_terracotta or white_terracotta for upper or sun-bleached faces.
- **Machiya / Uchiha dark walls:** Dark_oak_planks or stripped_dark_oak_log boards below and calcite plaster above. This is the classic koshikabe (half-wall) split at the 1–1.5 block line.

### Depth (each step adds one shadow line)
- **Frame:**
  - Posts: stripped spruce or dark oak logs at corners and every 3–5 blocks, standing 1 block proud of the plaster face, or plaster set 1 back between posts.
  - Floor band: a horizontal stripped log, or a dark_oak_slab line at each floor (every 3–4 blocks), sitting proud.
- **Windows:**
  - Glass or shoji set 1 block back from the wall face.
  - Sill: bottom slab or upside-down stair below. Lintel: upside-down stair above.
  - Shutters or lattice: spruce trapdoors, open vertical, on the outer face.
- **Stone base:** 1 block of stone or cobblestone, stepping out ½ block with a stone_brick_slab cap.
- **Eaves:**
  - Overhang 1 block on townhouses and 2 on temples, clan halls and the Residence.
  - Soffit: upside-down stairs or top slabs on the underside.
  - Rafter ends (taruki): fences or dark oak trapdoors under the eave edge.
- **Rooftop clutter:** Water_tank column on a 4-leg fence frame. Iron_bars or chain pipes down walls. Lightning_rod antennas. Parapet as stone_brick_wall or smooth_quartz_slab caps.

### Japanese roofs
- **Pitch [S]:**
  - 22.5° (low; townhouses): alternate bottom and top slabs.
  - 45°: stairs.
  - Stairs + slabs + upside-down stairs mixed in a 2–3 block repeat give in-between pitches.
  - Use odd widths so the ridge centres.
  - Footprints over about 15×15 need several roof sections.
  - Roof height should be 1–2 storeys for houses.
  - Joins form a V; hollow out unseen blocks (Minecraft Wiki roof guide).
- **Curve (sori):** Make the first 1–2 courses at the eave slabs (shallow), then switch to stairs toward the ridge (steeper). Add an eave lip: a bottom slab or upside-down stair one course below the first roof row, sticking out 1 block.
- **Irimoya (hip-and-gable):**
  - Build a hip on all four sides up to about ⅔ of the height.
  - Above that, set a small gable triangle in on the short ends, 1 block back from the hip line.
  - Face the triangle with planks or a lattice of trapdoors, with a ridge-cap row on top.
  - Use it for the Academy wings, clan halls, the Hyūga gate and the Naka Shrine.
- **Kirizuma (gable)** for plain houses. **Yosemune (hip)** for shops.
- **Ridge:** One row of full blocks or slabs along the top, capped with your kawara ridge cap. End each ridge with a raised cap (onigawara): a wall block + slab, or a ridge-cap end piece.
- **Colour use:**
  - Grey deepslate tile for ordinary houses and the Uchiha district.
  - Green copper for public buildings and the Academy.
  - Orange copper for shops and the Residence accents.
- **Machiya pent roof (hisashi):** A 1-block-deep slab or stair skirt roof at the first-floor line over the shopfront, running the full width.

### Machiya fronts
- **Koshi lattice:** On the ground floor, spruce or dark_oak fences, or vertical-open trapdoors, set 1 block in front of glass or shoji (Minka guide: "fences or iron bars in front of windows").
- **Mushiko window:** On a low upper floor, iron_bars or bamboo slats set into plaster.
- **Shop opening:** Noren over the door and a short awning. Lean bamboo-fence inuyarai at the wall base.
- **Signage:** A vertical sign board (banner or custom block) on the post beside the door.

### Lighting
- **Hidden light:** Use `minecraft:light` blocks (invisible, set level 10–15) in Python block lists. Place them inside eaves, under counters and behind shoji, so rooms glow without visible torches.
- **Visible light:**
  - Lanterns on chains under eaves, every 4–6 blocks along shop streets.
  - Paper lanterns at shop doors.
  - Stone lanterns at shrines and gardens.
  - Glowing shoji: shroomlight or a light block behind shoji or white stained glass.

### Streets and paths
- **Gradient chain [S]:** grass → dirt_path → coarse_dirt → gravel → andesite or cobble → polished stone → stone_bricks. Keep the centre cleaner and the edges rougher (4netplayers; Conquest guide).
- **Konoha main street (earthen beige):**
  - Centre: packed_mud and dirt_path.
  - Edges: coarse_dirt and gravel clusters.
  - Kerb: 1 row of polished_andesite or stone_brick_slab.
  - Gutter: 1-wide channel, ½ block lower (bottom slab of stone), covered by iron_bars or trapdoors at house doors.
- **Back lanes:** Gravel with stepping stones (smooth_stone_slab) and moss at the walls.
- **Utility poles:** Every 12–16 blocks on alternate sides, wires sagging 1 block mid-span.

### Terrain
- **Retaining walls (ishigaki):** Cobblestone, stone, andesite and tuff mixed, with a batter of 1 block in per 4 up (use stairs for the slope). Cap with a stone_brick_slab row.
- **Stairs on slopes:** Stone_brick or spruce stairs. Add a landing every 6–8 steps and handrails of fences or walls. Use slab ramps where rise is 1 per 2.
- **Terraces:** 3–5 blocks tall between village levels. The Rock's toe should rise in 2–3 terraces to the Academy and Residence.

### Vegetation
- **Canopy:** Konoha is a "leaf" village, so large broadleaf trees dominate. Use dark_oak and oak, with custom large canopies 15–25 high.
- **Pines:** Spruce logs with flat, cloud-pruned leaf pads. Dark oak trunks suit niwaki garden pines.
- **Accents:** Cherry trees sparingly at the shrine, Academy and riverside. Bamboo in clan gardens. Azalea or flowering azalea hedges. Moss carpet, ferns and short grass at wall bases.

## C. Lived-in interiors at 1 block = 1 m

### Real-world scale facts [S]
- **Tatami:** Edoma 88 × 176 cm, Kyoma 95.5 × 191 cm. **1 mat = 1 × 2 blocks.**
- **Room sizes:**
  - 4.5-mat room = 3 × 3 blocks (yojōhan).
  - 6-mat = 3 × 4.
  - 8-mat = 4 × 4.
  - Western bedrooms are about 6 tatami (9–10 m²); the master about 8 (e-housing).
- **Ceiling:** 2.4 m today, 2.1 m legal minimum, about 2.2 m in old homes. Use 3 blocks clear inside.
- **Genkan [S]:** A lower tiled tataki with a step up and a tall getabako shoe cabinet. About 1 m² in small flats. The door opens outward.
- **Kitchen [S]:** 2–4 burners with a fish-grill drawer under them. No big oven (microwave instead). A narrow tall fridge, a rice cooker and a range hood.
- **Bath [S]:** Unit bath about 160 × 200 cm with a deep tub, hand shower and floor drain. The toilet is almost always a separate room.
- **Washroom [S]:** The changing room (datsuijo) holds the sink vanity and a top-loading washing machine.
- **Balcony [S]:** About 1 m deep, with laundry and futon poles and the AC outdoor unit.
- **Washitsu [S]:**
  - Tokonoma: an alcove raised about 10–13 cm, holding a scroll, ikebana or incense.
  - Butsudan: often in the washitsu, sometimes in the tokonoma.
  - Kamidana: high on a wall.
  - Oshiire: futon closet 75–90 cm deep with fusuma.
  - Engawa: a wooden veranda.
  - Sources: tofugu; japan-experience; e-housing.

### Room lists
- **Single-person flat (10×8 outside, about 8×6 inside), Naruto-type:**
  - Genkan (1×2): stepped down, with getabako.
  - Kitchen strip (1×4) along the entry wall: sink, 2-burner stove, small fridge, one cabinet.
  - Unit bath (2×2: tub + sink) and toilet (1×1).
  - Main room (about 5×4): bed or futon, low table, desk, small TV, plants on the window sill.
  - Balcony (1-deep): laundry pole and washing machine.
- **Two-storey family house (9×7 inside per floor):**
  - Ground floor:
    - Genkan (2×2) with stair start.
    - LDK (about 5×4): kitchen run, dining table for 4, TV corner.
    - Washitsu (6-mat, 3×4) with tokonoma (1×2), butsudan and oshiire (1×2).
    - Washroom (2×2) with washer and sink, leading to the bath (2×2).
    - Toilet (1×2) under the stair.
  - First floor:
    - Two bedrooms (6-mat), each with oshiire or wardrobe.
    - Toilet.
    - Landing desk.
    - Balcony across the front for laundry and futon airing.
- **Shop-house (machiya, 5–6 wide × 12–15 deep):**
  - Ground floor:
    - Shop front (counter, display, noren).
    - Toriniwa: an earthen side passage (packed_mud floor) running to the back, holding the kitchen (kamado/stove, sink).
    - Raised tatami room behind the shop.
    - Tsuboniwa: a tiny light-well garden.
    - Bath and toilet at the back.
  - Upper floor: family washitsu, bedroom with oshiire, storage.

### One-line vanilla recipes
- **Genkan:** Floor of stone or polished_andesite bottom slabs, house floor a full block (½-block step). Getabako: a spruce block column with trapdoors on the face, flower pot on top.
- **Sink:** Cauldron (water-filled) in the counter, tripwire_hook or lever above as the tap (Minecraft Wiki furniture).
- **Stove:** Smoker (or furnace) under a counter top, campfire or iron trapdoor on top as burners, iron_trapdoor hood above, plus a light block.
- **Fish grill:** Iron_trapdoor on the smoker face.
- **Fridge:** 1×2 column, iron_block over a dispenser, iron_door on the front, button handle (Minecraft Wiki), or a white_concrete pair with an iron_trapdoor face.
- **Counters and cabinets:** Smooth_quartz_slab or spruce_slab top on barrels or planks with spruce or birch trapdoor fronts. Wall cupboards: trapdoors on the wall above the counter.
- **Rice cooker / kettle:** Decorated_pot or flower_pot on the counter. Microwave: a black or grey item-frame-faced block.
- **Kotatsu:** Spruce_slab (top) over a 2×2 of wool or carpet edges. Simpler: a 1–2 block spruce trapdoor table on a red carpet (heater glow from a light block underneath).
- **Low table (chabudai):** One spruce trapdoor (closed, upper half) or a bottom slab on the floor.
- **Zabuton cushions:** Carpet on the floor next to the low table, in red, brown or blue.
- **Futon:** White and light_blue carpet 1×2 on tatami, a bed for a usable spawn, folded futons stacked as wool in the oshiire.
- **Oshiire:** 1-deep × 2-wide recess, top shelf of a spruce_slab line at mid-height, front of birch or white trapdoors or your shoji block, wool stacks inside.
- **Tatami:** Your tatami_mat block laid 1×2 with seams staggered (never a 4-corner cross). Fallback: lime or yellow carpet bordered by a dark carpet strip.
- **Tokonoma:**
  - Alcove 1 deep × 2 wide, floor raised by a polished wood bottom slab.
  - Corner post: a dark oak or cherry log (tokobashira).
  - Hanging scroll: a banner or painting on the back wall.
  - Ikebana: a flower_pot with a flower or azalea on the slab.
- **Butsudan:** Dark_oak block with a dark_oak trapdoor pair as doors, a candle on top, a small gold item in an item frame.
- **Kamidana:** Spruce_slab shelf high on the wall with a small item frame and a white candle.
- **Tansu (chest of drawers):** 1–2 high barrels or spruce blocks faced with spruce trapdoors; a chest for real storage.
- **Bookshelf / shelf:** Bookshelf or chiseled_bookshelf, wall shelf of trapdoors or slabs with items in item frames.
- **Desk:** Spruce slab on fence or trapdoor legs, lantern or candle, item frame with map or paper, lectern for an open book.
- **Chair:** One stair, sides capped with signs or trapdoors; floor seating in washitsu uses carpets instead.
- **Ofuro tub:** 2×1 or 2×2 sunken one block, spruce or stripped_log rim, water, iron_trapdoor lid half-closed. Shower: tripwire_hook head + lightning_rod pipe.
- **Toilet:** Hopper (or white flower_pot on a quartz slab) with a quartz or birch trapdoor seat lid, button flush (Minecraft Wiki).
- **Washing machine:** White_concrete or smooth_quartz block, iron_trapdoor or glass_pane door face, button dial; set on the balcony or in the washroom.
- **Laundry pole:** Fence posts with chain or iron_bars rail, banners or white carpet on the rail as clothes and futons.
- **TV:** 2×1 black_concrete with item-frame screen or black stained glass on a low spruce cabinet (Minecraft Wiki).
- **Lighting:** Lantern on a chain, a candle cluster on a slab, a paper lantern beside the washitsu, light blocks in ceilings.
- **Plants:** Flower_pots on sills and counters, an azalea in a decorated_pot by the genkan, a bamboo pot in the tsuboniwa.
- **Shop counter:** Spruce stairs (upside-down) as the front, slab top, barrels and decorated_pots behind, item frames with goods, noren over the opening.
- **Clutter (lived-in):** Item frames with cups, books and scrolls. Carpet rugs offset from walls. Shoes (a brown carpet scrap) at the genkan. A calendar painting by the kitchen. Paper or bundle items on the desk.
