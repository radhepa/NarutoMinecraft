# Small talk, part A: the answer to "How's your day?" for proud residents. Standards, family name, competitive.
#! tags: personality=proud

## chat.personal  fn=reveal_character
Excellent, naturally. My persimmons took first prize at the market again. Third year running. Not that I'm counting.
My family has made tea bowls for five generations. I glazed one this morning that my grandfather would have signed.
I was second in the river race this morning. Second. I'll be training until it's first.
The neighbours have a new garden lantern. Stone, very nice. Mine's bigger. Older, too. I just thought you should know.
My son brought home top marks in calligraphy. Of course he did. I taught him to hold a brush before he could walk.
I don't do anything halfway. If I sweep the step, I sweep it so well people stop and look.
I've been told my grilled eel is the best on the street. I've also been told by people who haven't tried it. Same result.
I wore my grandmother's festival yukata to the shrine. It still fits perfectly. Some things are made to last.
I beat the tea house owner at shogi today. Two moves before the owner saw it coming. I'll remember that for weeks.
My sister thinks her dumplings are better than mine. Hers are fine. Mine are a tradition.
Someone said my garden was "nice". Nice! It took me nine years. Nice is a word for weather.
My family's name is carved on the old bridge. We helped build it. I polish the letters every spring.
I don't accept help with my roof. I've done it myself since I was fifteen. It hasn't leaked once. Not once.
I wrote a poem for the shrine contest. It's very good. I'm only saying so because it's true.
My husband says I'm too competitive. I'm not. I'm simply better at being competitive than he is.
I repainted the family crest on our gate. Took all morning. The gold has to be exactly right, or it looks cheap.
I never wear the same outfit twice in a week. People notice these things. I notice them, at least.
I was asked to judge the dango contest. I accepted, obviously. My standards are the reason they asked.
I'm teaching my nephew to fish. He caught one today. Small. I told him it was enormous. Don't repeat that.
I lost at cards to my brother. He cheated. I'm almost certain he cheated. We play again tomorrow.
My great-grandfather planted the cherry tree by the river. Everyone sits under it. Nobody knows. I know.
I made a kimono from scratch. Every stitch mine. If you see it at the festival, you'll know it's mine. It's the best one.
I don't brag. But my rice is the whitest on the lane, and people should know how much work that takes.
I've never been late in my life. I was early to my own birth, according to my mother.
The new family down the road asked me for gardening advice. Of course they did. I gave them a full list.
I keep my grandfather's medal in a frame by the door. I'm not showing off. It's simply where the light is best.
I won't say I'm the best cook on this street. I'll let the empty bowls say it.

Every year the Academy hangs the best calligraphy in the front hall. The names under them stay for a whole season.
The market street has a prize for the best stall sign each spring. Winning means something on that street. Trust me.
