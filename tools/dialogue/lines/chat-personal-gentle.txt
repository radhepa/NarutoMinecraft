# Small talk, part A: the answer to "How's your day?" for gentle residents. Soft-spoken, kind to animals and children.
#! tags: personality=gentle

## chat.personal  fn=reveal_character
A soft one. I found a baby sparrow under the eaves and put it back in the nest. The mother came back. I was so relieved.
I spent the morning brushing the old dog at the clinic. It falls asleep halfway through. Every time.
The little ones on my street brought me dandelions. I put them in a cup on the windowsill. They'll wilt. I don't mind.
I've been reading to my grandmother. She falls asleep in the middle of a chapter, and I just keep going softly.
The shrine's moss garden needed watering. You have to do it gently, or the moss gets upset. That's what I think, anyway.
A butterfly landed on my sleeve at the market and stayed for a whole street. I walked very slowly.
My daughter's learning to braid. She practises on my hair every evening. I look very strange. I love it.
I helped a child who was crying by the river. They'd lost a sandal. We found it together. Small things matter.
I made rice porridge for my husband. He's not ill, I just thought he looked tired. He ate two bowls.
There's a little dish of water by my door for the stray cats. Four come by now. They take turns, very politely.
I spent the afternoon pressing flowers for a friend who's moving away. Each one's from somewhere we went together.
I've been feeding the koi in the shrine pond. There's a shy one that won't come up unless the others leave.
There's an old tortoise in the Inuzuka garden that likes strawberries. I bring one every week. It knows my steps now.
I sang the neighbour's baby to sleep. The baby's mother cried a little. Three nights without sleep, she said.
Loud days aren't for me. Today was quiet. I spent it repotting violets.
I found a worm on the path and moved it to the grass. It's silly, I know. But the path is no place for a worm.
My son brought home a frog. We named it, fed it, and let it go by the pond. He was very brave about saying goodbye.
I sat with an old woman at the hospital who had no visitors. We talked about plums for an hour.
A puppy at the clinic has a new blanket. I knitted it. Too small, too pink, and the puppy adores it.
I made tea for the builders working on our lane. They're so loud, but they're nice. They said thank you three times.
There's a crow that brings me shiny things in exchange for bread. Today, a button. I'm keeping them all.
Evenings, I walk under the cherry trees. Even when they're not blooming, they're good company.
I spent the day helping my aunt make bean paste. My arms are tired. My heart is full.
A little girl asked me if flowers feel cold. I told her they wear their petals like blankets. She looked so satisfied.
The children in the hospital get letters from me. Little stories about animals. One wrote back with a drawing of a rabbit.
I don't cook much. But I make good tea. People come by just for the tea. I think. Or maybe the cat.
I cried at the fishmonger's today. A crab escaped and I cheered for it. It was caught again. I paid for it and let it go.

I work at the Inuzuka clinic, cleaning the kennels. The puppies are all teeth. I don't mind. I come home very happy.
I look after the little ones at the day house while their parents work. Today we drew clouds. Eleven clouds. All lovely.

I'm a medic. Today I set a broken arm and held a scared child's hand. I like the second part better.
On my last mission I found an injured fox in a trap. My squad waited while I freed it. They're good people.
I trained with the ninken today. I'm not good at the fighting part, but the dogs like me. That counts for something.

The shrine lets anyone leave a small bowl of rice for the birds on the first day of each month. It's lovely to see.
The Inuzuka clinic treats any animal, not just ninken. I've seen them bandage a duck.
On graduation day, the parents tie paper cranes to the tree by the Academy swing. For luck. It looks so pretty.

The baby birds under the shrine eaves keep falling out. I've been putting them back, but I'm not tall enough for the last one.
