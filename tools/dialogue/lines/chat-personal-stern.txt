# Small talk, part A: the answer to "How's your day?" for stern residents. Discipline, terse, cares by demanding more.
#! tags: personality=stern

## chat.personal  fn=reveal_character
Adequate. I rose at five, trained, worked, and ate plainly. That is how a day should be spent.
My son left his sandals in the hallway. Again. He will be cleaning every sandal in the house tomorrow.
I corrected the tea house menu. "Their" was wrong. Nobody else would have done it.
The children on my lane were throwing stones in the river. I made them pick up every stone and put it back. All of them.
I don't celebrate birthdays. A day is a day. My family celebrates mine anyway. I allow it. Once a year.
Sloppy footwork everywhere at the training grounds today. I said nothing. Today.
I sweep my step every morning. If you sweep only when it's dirty, you'll never notice when it starts.
I reprimanded a merchant for short-changing an old woman. The merchant paid her back, double. Good.
My father taught me to stand straight. I teach my children the same. They complain. Their backs will thank me.
Naps? No. Naps are for those who did not sleep properly. Sleep properly.
A Genin skipped warm-ups in front of me today. Pulled a muscle within five minutes. The lesson taught itself.
I finished my chores before dawn. The rest of the day is for work, and the evening is for reflection. Nothing else.
My wife says I could smile more. I smiled at the festival last year. Once is sufficient.
The neighbour's fence joints: I re-did them. They were wrong. Now they are not.
I do one hundred push-ups each morning. At my age, it's ninety. Next year, eighty. Never zero.
I keep my house the way my mother kept hers. Spotless. You can tell everything about a person from their kitchen.
My daughter came home with a scraped knee and a good mark. I asked about the mark first. Then I cleaned the knee.
Strong, bitter tea. That's how I make it. Sweet tea makes people soft.
I do not gossip. But I heard the bridge repairs are behind schedule, and someone should be held to account.
No alarm. I wake on my own. The body learns if you're strict with it.
I told my nephew his calligraphy was poor. He rewrote it thirty times. Now it's acceptable. He's proud. As he should be.
The neighbour's children now bow correctly. I taught them. They were doing it like ducks.
Some people waste the morning. I am not some people.
Finished a book on discipline. Mostly correct. The final chapter was soft.
My grandmother lived to ninety-seven on rice, pickles and no complaining. I intend to follow her example.
Sandals in a line by the door. Toes forward. You leave a house ready to return to it.
I don't like waste. Food, time, words. You'll notice I'm not wasting many now.
I helped the old woman on my lane with her roof. She thanked me. I told her not to climb up there again. She won't.
