# Small talk, part A: the answer to "How's your day?" for steadfast residents. Their own life, in their voice.
#! tags: personality=steadfast

## chat.personal  fn=reveal_character
Steady. Fixed my neighbour's gate, like I said I would. Took me a week to get to it, but it's done.
I promised my mother I'd visit every Sunday. Twelve years now. I haven't missed one.
Up at dawn, chores done, nothing broken. That's a good day in my book.
I've had the same pair of boots for nine years. Resoled them four times. Good boots don't quit, so I don't either.
Carried water for the old couple up the hill again. They try to pay me. I just keep coming back.
I don't say much at the meetings on my street. But when there's a roof to fix, people know who'll show up.
My son asked me what I'm good at. I said, "showing up." He looked disappointed. He'll understand later.
Same as yesterday. Up early, worked hard, ate rice. I don't need more than that.
I've walked the same route to work for fifteen years. The cobbler on the corner sets the shop clock by me.
I keep my word. Even when it's small. Especially when it's small. Small promises are where people learn to trust you.
My father built this house with his hands. I patch the roof every spring the way he showed me.
I helped dig out the drain on our lane after the rain. Nobody asked. Someone had to.
I'm not clever, but I'm reliable. My wife says that's rarer.
Finished my shift, walked the dog, fixed a chair. Tomorrow I'll do the same, and that suits me.
I lent a friend money years ago. They paid it back today, every ryo. I never doubted they would.
I've kept the same hours at my job since my first day. Some people think that's dull. I call it dependable.
I learned a long time ago that you don't wait for the right mood to do the work. You do the work and the mood follows.
The well at the end of my street gets stuck every winter. Every winter, I free it. It's become a kind of friendship.
I've been training the young ones on my street to split wood properly. Back straight, let the axe fall. No shortcuts.
I said I'd paint the fence at the shrine before the festival. Two coats done. One to go.
My daughter forgot her lunch, so I walked it to the Academy. Then went back to work. That's how the day went.
I keep a spare key for half the doors on my street. People trust me with them. I don't take that lightly.
I sat with my brother at the hospital last night. Didn't say much. Didn't need to. He's better today.
I don't like to complain. Rain is rain. Work is work. You put your coat on and get on with it.
Every morning I check the old woman next door has lit her stove. If there's no smoke, I knock.
My grandfather's motto was "one more bucket". I think about that whenever I want to stop early.
I've stood in the same spot at every Leaf Festival for twenty years, by the third lantern post. People find me there.
Not much to tell. I did my part. That's enough.

My sensei told me the best shinobi are the ones who come back and do it again tomorrow. I try to be that.

## chat.personal  fn=worldbuild
On our street, whoever finds a fallen tree after a storm clears it. You don't wait for the village to send someone.
When a house burns in the Leaf, the neighbours rebuild it in a week. It's been that way since the founders.
The gate guards change shift at six and six. Rain or snow. In all my years I've never seen it late.
