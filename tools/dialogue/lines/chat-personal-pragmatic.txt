# Small talk, part A: the answer to "How's your day?" for pragmatic residents. Costs, practicality, no nonsense.
#! tags: personality=pragmatic

## chat.personal  fn=reveal_character
Profitable. Sold the last of the winter radishes before they went soft. Timing is everything.
I fixed the roof myself. A carpenter wanted forty ryo. Two hours and a ladder, and I've saved enough for a month of tea.
Rice, I buy in fifty-pound sacks. Cheaper by the sack. My wife says the pantry looks like a fortress. Good. It is one.
I spent the morning comparing tea prices on the market street. Third stall from the bridge is two ryo cheaper. Same leaves.
Sandals, I mend myself. Leather, needle, wax. Why would I pay someone for something I can do in ten minutes?
My son wants a kunai set for his birthday. I said: earn half, I'll pay half. He's been weeding gardens for a week.
I grow my own green onions in a pot on the windowsill. Cut them, they grow back. Best value in the Leaf.
I sold my old cart and bought a better one with the money and a bit more. Now I carry twice the load. Simple maths.
The festival's coming. Everyone's buying new yukata. I'm wearing last year's. It still fits and nobody remembers.
There's a shortcut through the alley behind the bathhouse. Saves four minutes a day. That's a day a year.
Cook once, eat three times. Moonday stew is Fireday soup and Waterday rice topping. Waste nothing.
Never buy fish in the afternoon. The good ones go by eight. That's the rule I live by.
A merchant tried to sell me a "lucky" teapot today for triple the price. Luck isn't a glaze.
I reuse the water from washing rice on the garden. The plants don't care. The water bill does.
I lent my ladder to three neighbours this week. Next time, I'm charging in vegetables.
I learned to sharpen my own knives. The smith charges three ryo a blade. That's a dango every time.
Every coin under ten ryo goes in a jar. At the end of the season, it pays for a festival evening. Small change adds up.
I don't buy things I can borrow, and I don't borrow things I'll need twice.
My father taught me: if it's broken, fix it; if it can't be fixed, sell the parts. He sold a lot of parts.
I spent the day planning next month's meals. Saves time and ryo and arguments.
Same coat every season. Layers underneath. One coat, four seasons. Practical.
I patched my roof with tiles from the old shed. They don't match. They don't leak, either.
My house is four streets from the market and two from the river. That's why I chose it. Location, location, fish.
The new tea house charges eight ryo a cup. The old one charges five and it's closer. I'm loyal to my wallet.
I've started drying my own herbs on the porch. Saves money, and the house smells like a forest.
I go to the bathhouse at the end of the day when it's half price. Same water. Fewer people.
I'm teaching my daughter to haggle. She got a ryo off a bag of peaches today. I've never been prouder.
Two lists: things I want, things I need. The second list gets bought. The first gets slept on.

## chat.personal  rank=civilian  fn=reveal_character
I run a stall on the market street. Shinobi pay well, but they're never home. Civilians pay less, but they come back.
I sell firewood. Winter's my season. I spend all summer chopping and all winter counting.

## chat.personal  rank=!civilian  fn=reveal_character
A D-rank pays eighty ryo if you're quick. Two in a morning, and the rent's paid. Weeding's not glamorous. Rent isn't either.
I buy my kunai in bulk from the smith. Discount for twenty. Sharp is sharp.
I take the missions nobody wants. Fewer squads competing, and the clients are grateful. Grateful clients tip.
Soldier pills cost sixty ryo. I carry one, never use it, and sell it back if it's close to expiry. Don't tell the Desk.

## chat.personal  fn=worldbuild
Market Day once a week is when the farmers bring their best. Prices drop by noon. That's when I buy.
The pawn shop gives half the price of anything new. Half's better than a cupboard full of junk.
The Leaf charges no gate tax on food coming in. That's why the farmers come here instead of the towns.
Shops close by evening. Ichiraku stays open later. That's why it's always busy. Smart business.
