# Small talk, part A: the answer to "How's your day?" for meticulous residents. Their own life, in their voice.
#! tags: personality=meticulous

## chat.personal  fn=reveal_character
Productive. I relabelled every jar in my kitchen. Then I relabelled the labels. The first set was crooked.
I wake at 5:40 exactly. Not 5:30, that's too early, and not 6:00, that's lazy. 5:40 is correct.
I've finally finished alphabetising my spices. Cinnamon was in the "S" section. I don't know how long that went on.
My neighbour borrowed my broom and returned it facing the wrong way. I'm choosing to say nothing. I'm saying it to you.
I've written tomorrow's schedule on a card. Nine items. There's a slot for "unexpected things". It's eleven minutes long.
This morning I ironed my handkerchiefs. All of them. Even the ones I don't use. Especially those.
Three grams short! I measured my rice this morning, and the bag was light. I'll be having a word with the grocer.
I keep a ledger of every ryo I spend. Today: tea, two ryo. Bread, four. A sweet I didn't need, one. I've noted the sweet.
Sock drawer: reorganised by colour, then by thickness. The thickness system won.
My husband stacks the bowls largest at the top. Twelve years married, and I still restack them every night.
I spent the morning re-rolling my scrolls so all the seals face the same way. You don't know how satisfying that is.
There are four hundred and twelve tiles on my roof. I counted after the storm. Four hundred and eleven now. I'll fix it.
I don't like surprises. I like plans. A surprise is just a plan that someone forgot to tell you about.
Every pencil in the house is sharpened. Then I lined them up by length. Now I'm afraid to use them.
I sorted my letters by date received, then by sender. I've received a lot of letters from my mother.
Three minutes, twenty seconds. Perfect. Yesterday's was three-forty. I don't talk about yesterday.
Laundry goes in thirds. Halves leave a crease down the middle, and I won't have that.
I swept the street in front of my house. Then the street in front of my neighbour's, so mine would look right.
Ask me any date. My calendar has every festival, birthday and holiday for the next two years.
I made a chart of when the shops open and close. The dango shop never matches it. I suspect the owner of whims.
I spent an hour straightening the picture frames in the tea house. The owner didn't ask. The owner didn't stop me.
My handwriting has been the same since I was nine. I practised until it couldn't change. Some things should stay put.
That clock in the square runs four minutes fast. I've told three people. Nobody's done anything.
I wrote a list of my favourite things. Then I ranked it. Number one is a well-made list.
It's three hundred and sixty steps to the market. If it's more, I've taken a wrong turn or I'm tired.
I repainted the trim on my door. The colour's called "pine shade". It's one shade lighter than before. Nobody noticed.
Receipts. I keep all of them. In boxes, by season. My daughter says I could build a second house out of them.
My garden rows are exactly a forearm apart. The carrots are thriving. I credit the forearm.
I spent my day off reorganising the drawers at my sister's place. She was out. She's back now. She is not pleased.

I tailor uniforms. A Chunin vest needs fourteen pockets, and every one must sit level. I check them with a ruler.

I check my pouch three times before every mission: kunai, wire, tags, bandages, in that order. My squad calls it the ritual.
I write mission reports the evening I get back. My squad hands theirs in three days late. I've stopped commenting. Mostly.
I keep my kunai sharpened to the same angle. My sensei said it doesn't matter. It matters to me.

The market's weights are checked by an inspector every season. A stamped weight means you can trust the scale.
