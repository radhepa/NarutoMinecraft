# A sensei's advice after a fight, keyed by the habit the fight showed. A neutral mentor voice until
# the cast is written. Pools are combat.sensei_tip.<habit>.
#! kind: cycle
#! fn: teach

## combat.sensei_tip.spam_same_jutsu
The same jutsu three times running? By the third, anyone worth fighting has a counter ready.
A technique is a word, not a sentence. Mix it with throws and footwork so it lands as a surprise.
You leaned on one jutsu the whole fight. Learn a second, even a small one, and they'll never settle.
Watch their face the next time you cast it twice. They stop flinching. That's your cue to change.

## combat.sensei_tip.never_guards
You never once raised your hands. Blocking costs you nothing but a moment, and it halves the hurt.
Attack is half the work. Hold your guard while you wait for an opening, not after you've been hit.
Your opponent swung freely because you let them. Raise a block and make them think twice.
If you can't dodge it, block it. If you block it at the last instant, you can turn it right back.

## combat.sensei_tip.never_dodges
You stood and took every hit. A quick dash to the side would have spared you most of them.
Dodge when you see the shoulder drop or the hands start weaving. Standing still is a choice too.
Your feet stayed planted all fight. Light feet, short dashes. Make them chase empty air.
A dodge at the very instant of the strike is the substitution's doorway. Practise the timing.

## combat.sensei_tip.out_of_chakra
You ran dry halfway through. Chakra is a meal, not a feast: ration it.
Before a fight, meditate a moment and let your chakra fill. After, a soldier pill if you must.
Your jutsu were fine. Your timing wasn't. You spent everything before the fight had really begun.
When you're low, fight with your hands and feet and let the chakra creep back. Don't force signs.

## combat.sensei_tip.too_far
You kept your distance the whole time. Safe, yes, but you never threatened them once.
Throwing from far off is fine for a start. Then close in, or they'll keep picking you apart.
Stay that far back and they choose when the fight happens. Step in and take the choice from them.
Your shuriken were good. Follow them in next time, while they're still busy dodging.

## combat.sensei_tip.too_close
You crowded them so close you couldn't see what their hands were doing. Give yourself a step.
Up close you can't dodge, only trade blows. Back off a pace after a combo and reset.
Hugging an opponent only works if you're the stronger one. Today you weren't. Use the space.
A good fighter breathes in and out of range like a tide. You stayed on the beach.

## combat.sensei_tip.ignores_tells
They raised their arm before every heavy strike. Did you notice? Watch for it next time.
Every enemy shows you what's coming. A wind-up, a flash, a step back. Learn to read it.
You looked at their weapon. Look at their shoulders and their feet instead. They speak first.
When an attack glows or the hands start weaving, that's your warning. Move then, not after.

## combat.sensei_tip.good_parry_streak
Three parries in a row. That's not luck, that's reading. Keep doing exactly that.
You turned their best strikes back on them. When they stagger, strike right away.
Your timing on the block was sharp today. Plenty of Chunin never manage that.
Good parrying. Next step: follow each one with a combo before they recover.
