# Small talk, part A: the life of each rank, as the speaker lives it. Academy-rank speakers are children (another file).

## chat.rank  rank=civilian  fn=reveal_character
Shop opened at seven, closed at six, and in between I sold forty bowls of rice. Not a single jutsu involved.
I watch the squads leave through the gate every morning on my way to the stall. I always wonder which ones I'll see come back tired.
People think civilians just sit about while the shinobi work. You try hauling fish crates at dawn.
My stall is right by the Mission Desk road. I can tell how a mission went by how fast a squad walks home.
I'm the third generation of my family to run this shop. None of us could mould chakra. All of us could make change.
My son wants to join the Academy. I'm proud. I'm terrified. Mostly I'm buying him a lot of rice.
I tried to throw a kunai once at the festival stall. Hit the stall owner's hat. They gave me a prize just to make me stop.
My neighbour's a Jonin. Lovely person, never home. I water the plants and they bring me odd fruit from far places.
I do the books for the hardware shop. Shinobi buy wire by the mile. I've stopped asking what for.
The best thing about being a civilian? When the alarm bell rings, my job is to go home and lock the door. Simple.
I launder Chunin vests for half the shinobi on this street. You'd never believe what ends up in those pockets.
I've lived in the Leaf my whole life and never once climbed a wall without a ladder. I'm very proud of my ladder.
When I was young I wanted to be a shinobi. Now I bake. Some mornings, shaping dough feels like a kind of jutsu.
My daughter's a Genin now. She tells me nothing about her missions. I find out from the laundry.
I sell sandals. Shinobi wear through three pairs a season. Bless them, they pay my rent.
Some shinobi come in and pay with odd coins from far villages. I keep a jar of them. It's like having a little map.
I sweep the street outside my shop and find shuriken in the gutter. I keep a box for them. Someone always comes back.
Running a tea house means hearing everything and repeating nothing. That's the civilian's code, more or less.
I don't have a headband, but I've got a good knife and the sharpest tongue on the market street.
My husband's a carpenter. He fixes what the training grounds break. There's always a lot to fix.
The farmers outside the wall are civilians too. They grow every grain of rice this village eats. Never forget that.
I deliver letters. On a good day I can walk the whole village twice. Shinobi do it in ten minutes, over the roofs. Show-offs.
Every time a squad brings a merchant back safe, the merchant comes to my inn. I hear the story twice: once scared, once brave.
I don't understand half of what the shinobi say. "C-rank", "B-rank". I just know an A means they'll be gone a while.
I had a ninja come in to buy flour, and they paid by walking up the wall to get the top sack. Very helpful.
I can't make a clone, but I can make three dinners at once. I think that's a fair trade.
I grow vegetables for the Akimichi kitchen. They say my carrots taste of sunshine. They're being polite. They're right.
I'm a civilian born to shinobi parents. Couldn't mould a scrap of chakra. My mother said I'd have a longer life. She was right so far.
I sit on the market council. Mostly we argue about where carts may stop. It's very important. To us.
My fishing boat's older than most of the Jonin. Still floats. Still catches more than they do.
My job is to keep the bath hot and the gossip cold. I'm good at the first one.

## chat.rank  rank=civilian  fn=worldbuild
Civilians run the Leaf's daily life: the shops, the farms, the baths. The shinobi protect it. That's the deal.
Shinobi pay the same prices as everyone. No discount for a headband. Some of them still try.
The market council is all civilians. Even the Hokage has to ask us before moving a stall.
On festival nights the civilians run everything: the stalls, the music, the lanterns. The shinobi just eat.
Civilian children can enrol in the Academy like anyone else. Most come out with nothing but good manners and strong legs.
When a squad doesn't come home, the whole street knows within the hour. The shops close early. Nobody says why.
Civilian families keep shrines for their shinobi neighbours too. We light a candle when they leave on a long mission.
Most of the shops in the Leaf are civilian-run, even the weapon shop's front. The forge is the only shinobi-run part.
The Leaf pays farmers in good times and bad. In return, the farms feed the village through any winter.
Some civilian families have run the same shop since the founding. The noodle stalls have the longest memory of all.

## chat.rank  rank=civilian  fn=teach
If a shop's shutter is half down, the owner will still sell to you. All the way down means go home and come back at seven.
Market Day is once a week. Farmers come in from outside the wall with the freshest produce. Prices drop by noon.
If you want work without a headband, ask the shops. Ichiraku, the grocer, the flower shop and the forge all take part-timers.
The pawn shop will take what you don't need for half its price. Don't expect more. They've heard every story.

## chat.rank  rank=genin  fn=reveal_character
Weeded three gardens today. Three. My sensei says it builds character. My back says it builds blisters.
We spent the morning chasing a lost cat. Third time this month. Same cat. I think it enjoys the attention.
My squad almost finished a D-rank without arguing. Almost. We argued about the last bucket.
I got my first proper mission pay today. Ninety ryo. I bought ramen for the whole squad. Now I have six ryo.
My sensei made us carry logs up and down the training ground for two hours. I can't feel my arms. Worth it, apparently.
Painting a fence is a D-rank. Who knew? We did the whole thing in an hour. Our sensei did a different one in five minutes.
My teammate's always late. Every single morning. We started meeting fifteen minutes earlier, and they're still late.
I tried to impress my sensei with a new jutsu. It fizzled. My sensei laughed and showed me where I was wrong.
We escorted an old merchant to the farms and back. Long walk. The merchant told us the same joke four times.
I fell in the river during a fishing D-rank. My squad laughed. I caught the biggest fish anyway. Ha.
My sensei says teamwork matters more than talent. I'm talented and terrible at teamwork. I'm working on it.
My squad has a code word for "the sensei's watching". We use it so much now it doesn't mean anything.
I wear my headband to bed sometimes. Don't tell anyone. I'm still not used to having one.
The Mission Desk gave us a babysitting job. Three toddlers. Hardest mission of my life. I'm not joking.
I've been practising tree walking. I can get halfway up now before I slip. Yesterday it was a third.
My squad got our first C-rank and the whole thing was carrying boxes. Still. A C! I'm putting it on my wall.
Some days I feel like a real shinobi. Today I picked up rubbish by the river for four hours. Not one of those days.
We trained until sunset. My sensei bought us dango. I think that's the reward system. I'm learning.
My teammates and I have a bet on who'll make Chunin first. The loser buys ramen for a month.
I learned to tell how heavy a crate is by looking. That's from D-ranks, not training. Very useful skill, honestly.
I spent all morning trying to catch a bird that escaped from the tea house. It sat on my head when I gave up.
Got a scolding from my sensei for showing off a trick during a mission. Fair. I still think it was a good trick.
I helped rebuild a farmer's fence. The farmer gave us a sack of potatoes. Best pay I've had.
I can throw four shuriken at once now. Two hit. One got stuck in a tree. One's missing. We're looking.
My parents still treat me like a child even with the headband. I'm a shinobi! Who also has to be home by dinner.
My sensei can tell when I've skipped warm-ups just by watching me walk. It's very annoying.
Our squad's first mission outside the wall was a delivery to the waystation. I've never been so far. Didn't see a single bandit.
I thought Genin life would be all battles. It's mostly gardens, cats and helping people move furniture.
My squad finally learned to read each other's hand signals. Took months. Now we can be rude to each other silently.
We had a squad sleepover at our sensei's after a late mission. Our sensei snores. Loudly. Like a bear.
My teammate's mother makes the best rice balls. We always take that teammate's lunch. Team effort.
I'm saving for a better kunai pouch. Eight more D-ranks. Maybe nine if I buy dango.
I asked my sensei why we do D-ranks. The answer: "So the village knows your face before it needs your fists."

## chat.rank  rank=genin  fn=worldbuild
Every Genin squad is three students and a Jonin sensei. You do everything together. Everything.
D-ranks are the village's chores: weeding, deliveries, lost pets, fence painting. The Leaf runs on them.
Genin get their missions from the Mission Desk on the Residence's ground floor. You line up with your squad.
The Chunin Exams happen in spring and autumn. Squads enter together or not at all.
A sensei can refuse to put a squad forward for the exams. Mine says we'll go when we're ready. I'm ready now!
The headband's metal plate is pressed with the Leaf's symbol at the smithy. They're all the same. Feels special anyway.
There's an unspoken rule that the newest Genin squad gets the muddiest D-rank. It's a kind of welcome.
Genin squads keep a log of every mission. Ours has eighty-three entries. Fifty are about weeds.
Senseis can train their students at any ground they like. Ground 3 is the most popular. The stumps are famous.

## chat.rank  rank=genin  fn=teach
The Mission Desk is open from eight till six. Come early. The good D-ranks go first.
Training before a mission helps, but not too much. Exhausted Genin make mistakes.
A D-rank pays sixty to a hundred and fifty ryo. Enough to eat well and save a little.
If a mission's going badly, you go back and report. Pride doesn't count as a reason to keep going.

## chat.rank  rank=chunin  fn=reveal_character
Desk duty all morning. I stamped two hundred mission forms. My hand has become a stamp.
I lead my first squad of Genin next week. I'm more nervous than I was in my own exams.
I spent the day teaching a squad how to set a camp. One of them set their tent on fire. Learning.
I took the Chunin Exams twice before passing. I tell every Genin that. It helps more than any advice.
The vest is warm in winter and terrible in summer. Fourteen pockets and I still can't find my keys.
I'm on the Mission Desk rotation this month. You learn a lot about a village from what it asks for help with.
I escorted a merchant caravan to the border village and back. Six days, no trouble. That's the best kind.
I proctored a written exam at the Academy. Saw two cheating attempts and one very creative sandwich.
Some days I'm a leader, some days I'm a clerk. Today I was both before lunch.
I make the Genin squads I lead do warm-ups whether they like it or not. They do not like it.
I got my first B-rank. I'm not allowed to talk about it. I'm allowed to say I'm very tired.
My younger brother's a Genin now. He's on a squad I trained with once. It's strange watching from the other side.
Sometimes I miss D-ranks. You weed a garden, the garden's weeded. Simple. Leading a squad is never that simple.
I filled in for a sick Jonin sensei for a week. The squad asked when their sensei was coming back. Every hour.
I keep a little book of every mistake I've made on a mission. It's thick. I read it before every new one.
I spent the night on wall watch. The stars are beautiful from the top. The cold isn't.
I was given a squad of three loud Genin. I have learned to speak in a whisper so they have to stop and listen.
I was sent to teach a class at the Academy on wire traps. The children wanted to know if I'd ever fought a bear.
The hardest part of being a Chunin is deciding. On a mission, someone has to choose, and now that's me.
I wrote my first report as a squad leader. Eight pages. My Jonin said it should have been two.
I spent a week guarding a farm against wild boars. They came on the last night. I'll be telling that story for years.
I passed the exams last autumn. My parents still introduce me as "the Chunin". It's lovely. And a bit embarrassing.
My Jonin sensei still checks on me after every mission. I'm a Chunin! It's nice, though.
I train new Genin in chakra control twice a week. They're bad at it. I was worse.
I ran messages for the Hokage's office all day. Up and down the Residence stairs. I've decided stairs are a jutsu.
I'm saving for my own apartment. Shinobi pay's good when you get C-ranks, but rent on the main street is steep.
I volunteered to help with the next Chunin Exams. Watching from the other side is harder than taking them.

## chat.rank  rank=chunin  fn=worldbuild
Chunin lead squads, staff the Mission Desk, teach at the Academy and watch the wall. Most of the village's work is ours.
The Chunin vest has scroll pockets on the chest. Old shinobi say that's where your life's work goes.
A Chunin can be promoted to Jonin by the Hokage. There's no exam for that, just years and a good record.
The Chunin Exams are held in spring and autumn. Squads from other villages come, and the whole Leaf turns out to watch the finals.
Chunin on wall duty take four-hour watches. You learn to love tea and hate wind.
Every Chunin in the Leaf takes a shift at the Mission Desk sooner or later. It's how you learn what the village needs.

## chat.rank  rank=chunin  fn=teach
The Mission Desk opens at eight and closes at six. Bring your squad and your report. In that order.
C-rank missions pay three to six hundred ryo, but they go beyond the wall. Prepare properly.
If a squad comes in hurt, they go straight to the hospital. The report can wait. That's a rule.

## chat.rank  rank=jonin  fn=worldbuild
Jonin take on the hardest missions, lead the best squads, and teach the newest Genin. Some do all three in a week.
A Jonin sensei chooses whether a new squad passes their first test. Many don't. They go back to the Academy.
Jonin meet in a hall at the Residence before big missions. Quiet room. Very good tea.
Some Jonin specialise: medics, trackers, sealing experts. The Leaf needs every kind.
A Jonin's flak vest is the same as a Chunin's. What changes is how long you've worn it.

## chat.rank  rank=jonin  fn=teach

Pack light, pack smart. A kunai, a wire, a bandage, a ration. Everything else is weight.
A good shinobi eats well before a mission. Ichiraku's ramen restores a little chakra. Don't scoff at it.
