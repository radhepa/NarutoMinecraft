## chat.personal  fn=reveal_character
Busy keeping an eye on things. The children on my street were playing too near the river again. I moved them to the yard.
I stayed up until my daughter got home from training. She's sixteen and says I fuss. I do. I'll keep doing it.
Every lock on my street: checked last night. Twice. Habit. Nobody complains when the thieves go elsewhere.
I walked the old man from the tea house home. The lane's dark past the bend. It's ten minutes. Why wouldn't I?
I put a railing on our steps after my wife slipped last winter. Now I'm wondering about the neighbours' steps.
My little brother's on his first long mission. I've been to the gate three times today to see if the squad's back.
There's a first-aid kit in every room of my house. My husband says three is enough. Five is enough. I'm at six.
The ice on the pond looks solid but it isn't yet. I've told every child on the lane. Some of them twice.
That stray dog by the market has a sore paw. I wrapped it. Now it follows me home. That's fine.
I don't sleep well when the weather turns. I lie there listening for the wind on my neighbours' roofs.
I taught my son to whistle for help. Three short, one long. Every child on my street knows it now.
I walked my sister to the hospital for her check-up. She's fine. I'm still worried. That's my job.
A cart nearly hit a kid on the main street this morning. I had a word with the driver. A long word.
There's a lantern lit in my window at night so anyone walking home can see the corner. It's a small thing.
Every time there's thunder, I go round to the widow next door and sit with her. She hates storms.
I made the kids on my street promise to walk to the Academy in pairs. They roll their eyes. They do it.
I patched a hole in the fence by the river. Someone's toddler would've been through it in a week.
I've learned the name of every child on my street, just in case. I'd want someone to know mine.
My mother lives alone now. I visit before work and after work. She says I hover. She also leaves the door open.
I chased off a fox that was eyeing my neighbour's chickens. It came back. I'll be up tonight again.
A spare scarf goes with me everywhere. It's never for me.
I asked the gate guards how many came in today. They told me. It's not my job. I just like to know.
After every rain, I check the river level. If it's up past the second post, I warn the houses on the bank.
I followed a lost child around the market for half an hour until the parents turned up. Wasn't going to leave the poor thing.
My son's sensei sends a note after every mission now. I may have asked for that. Politely. Loudly.
That old bridge in the dark worries me. Half the boards are loose. I walk across it first and test them.
My sandals sleep by the bed. If anyone shouts, I'm out the door. My wife thinks I'm ridiculous.
