# Small talk from residents of no clan: clanless shinobi with ordinary family names, and civilian families.

# -- clanless shinobi ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## chat.clan  group=clanless  fn=reveal_character
No compound, no crest, no elders telling me how to bow. Just a headband and a name my grandmother sold rice under.
My family were farmers for six generations. I'm the first to throw a kunai, and they still don't know which end is sharp.
Nobody expects anything of a clanless Genin. That's the best head start there is.
I used to envy the clan kids their techniques. Then I watched one of them get lectured at the Hyuga gate for twenty minutes over a bow.
My jutsu are the ones anybody can learn. I just learned them harder.
I've got no clan day, so I made my own. Every year on the day I graduated, I buy myself the most expensive bowl at Ichiraku.
Sometimes I walk past the Senju hall and wonder what it's like to have a tree older than your family's memory. Then I go home to my landlady's cat.
My mother runs a laundry by the river. She irons my flak vest every week and tells the customers I'm in the guard, which is close enough.
A clan teaches you who you are. Being clanless, you have to work it out yourself, and I think I got a good answer.
When I made Chunin, the whole apartment block threw a party on the landing. Nobody had a crest; everybody had a pot of something.
The clan kids have someone to teach them their family's way. Mine was the library, an old Jonin and a lot of bruises.
My squad was an Inuzuka, a Hyuga and me. I was the one who kept the maps, carried the rice and got us home.

## chat.clan  group=clanless  fn=worldbuild
Most of the Leaf's shinobi are clanless. The clans are famous, but it's ordinary families who fill the Mission Desk queue every morning.
Clanless shinobi live in the apartment blocks near the Residence. You can tell which flats are ours by the laundry: all headbands and no crests.
The great clans have their quarters. We have the noodle shops, the bath house and the bench outside the Mission Desk, and I'd say we got the better deal.
A clanless Genin can learn a lesser technique from a friendly clan, if they earn it. I'm working on the Nara. They're hard to impress, and very slow to say so.
Clan days, the clanless shinobi cover the gate and the patrols so the clans can celebrate. Then they cover ours on New Year. It evens out.
There's a bar near the barracks where the clanless Jonin drink. No crests on the walls, just a board with every name that's ever made Jonin from an ordinary family.

## chat.clan  group=clanless  fn=reveal_character,react
I'll admit it, I'm proud of it. Every rank I have, I got without anyone's grandfather putting in a word.
Some evenings I'd like to go home to a big table and twenty cousins. Then I remember my flat is quiet, and I make tea, and it's fine.
People ask me which clan I'm from all the time. I tell them the Leaf, and it's never once been wrong.

You're one of us, {player}: no crest, no compound, no rules about how deep to bow. Make your own name; it's the only kind we get.
Clanless and in the Academy? Good. Learn from all of them. The clans only teach their own, but we can watch everyone.
Don't let the clan kids make you feel small. Half the Hokage's guard grew up in an apartment block, same as you.

## chat.clan  group=clanless  fn=hook
The old Jonin in the flat below mine has stopped coming to the bench outside the Mission Desk. It's not like them. Someone should knock.
A clanless Genin from my block keeps getting turned down for squad trials. I'd like someone with a clan to vouch for the kid once.

## chat.clan  group=clanless  fn=worldbuild,reveal_character
Our family's only heirloom is a bent kettle that went through three wars and a flood. We still use it. The tea tastes of history and rust.
I joke that my family's secret technique is getting up early. It's not a joke. It's why I made Chunin.
My grandfather says clans are like big trees. Fine to sit under, but you'll never see the sky.

# -- civilians -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## chat.clan  group=civilian  fn=reveal_character
We're not shinobi in this family. We bake. Every Genin squad that ever came back from a mission has eaten one of our buns.
I've lived next door to a shinobi family for twenty years. You stop jumping when someone lands on your roof at night. Mostly.
My daughter wanted to go to the Academy. We talked her round to the tea house instead. Some days I wonder if we did right.
My son made Genin last spring. Nobody in our family had so much as touched a kunai. I polish his headband when he isn't looking.
The village needs its shinobi, but it runs on its grocers, its cooks and its bath keepers. I'm proud of my apron.
Civilians don't get clan days, but we've got Market Day, and nobody's ever had to bow to a cabbage.
Our street is all civilian families: a cobbler, a laundress, two cooks and me. We share one garden and the arguments that come with it.
I was a little afraid of shinobi as a child. Then a Chunin carried my grandmother home from the market in the rain, and I wasn't anymore.
My grandfather says the Leaf was a farming village before it was a shinobi one. He likes to remind the Jonin of that at the bath house.
I can't do a single hand sign, but I can carry four trays of tea up three flights without spilling. We all have our training.

## chat.clan  group=civilian  fn=worldbuild
About half the Leaf is ordinary people: shopkeepers, farmers, carpenters and children who'll never throw a shuriken in their lives.
Civilian families live on the market street, the residential streets and down by the river. The shinobi are everywhere else, and on the roofs.
The Academy takes anyone with the will to try. Most civilian children try for a year, and most come home and help in the family shop.
Civilians pay the same ryo as anyone at Ichiraku. Shinobi just order more.
When the village is quiet, nobody notices the shopkeepers. When the shops shut for a festival, everybody notices.
We have our own customs: the first loaf of the year goes to the shrine, the first fish to the oldest neighbour, the first tea to whoever came furthest.
The civilian families of the riverside have a rowing race every summer. No jutsu allowed. The shinobi bet on it anyway.
Every civilian street has a shinobi family on it somewhere. They keep us safe; we keep them fed. That's the bargain, and it's a good one.

## chat.clan  group=civilian  fn=reveal_character,react
Shinobi land on my roof to cut across the street. I leave a sign saying "please walk on the left tiles". The good ones do.
My neighbour is a Jonin. We swap pickles and gossip over the fence, and I've never once asked what the job's like.
Sometimes I'm glad I'll never be called to the gate in the middle of the night. Sometimes I'm a little envious. Both are true.

## chat.clan  group=civilian  fn=hook
The cobbler on our street has gone quiet since the shop next door closed. I keep meaning to bring round some soup; maybe you could come too.
Our street's well bucket keeps going missing, and turns up on a different roof every week. We suspect a Genin. We'd like it back.

## chat.clan  group=civilian  fn=worldbuild,reveal_character
My family has kept the same stall for a hundred years. Hokage come and go; the turnip price is eternal.
I grew up thinking the Hokage Rock was where the Hokage slept. My mother let me think so until I was nine.
Our family's training is the abacus. My little sister can add faster than a Chunin can throw, and she reminds them often.
We civilians remember the shinobi who were kind to us as children. You'd be surprised how long that kindness lasts.

## chat.clan  group=civilian  fn=worldbuild
Civilian children who leave the Academy still keep their practice headbands. Half the shopkeepers on the market street have one in a drawer.
Our neighbourhood association meets at the tea house every Seventh Day. We argue about drains for an hour and then eat cake.
When the gate bell rings for a squad coming home, the whole riverside steps out to look. We don't know them all, but we wave anyway.
A civilian wedding at the shrine has no clan crests and no rules about bows. Just a lot of family and far too much sweet rice.

## chat.clan  group=clanless  fn=worldbuild
Clanless families pass down a headband instead of a crest. Mine has three names scratched inside it, and room for a fourth.
