# Small talk about where you're standing: what the place is, its regulars, and where to go from here.

# -- the Academy ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## chat.place  district=academy  fn=worldbuild
The swing on the big tree out front has been there longer than any of the instructors. Every child in the Leaf has fought over it.
The Academy yard is all target posts and dust. On a still afternoon you can hear shuriken thunking into wood from the main street.
The big fire sign over the entrance is repainted every spring by the senior class. It's a privilege, and they still spill the paint.
Classes run nine to noon on weekdays. By half past twelve the yard's empty and the dango stall down the road is full.
The janitor's been here forty years and knows every loose floorboard. That's how the late pupils get caught.
Graduation photos line the corridor by the staff room. Look closely and you'll find Jonin who used to have terrible haircuts.

## chat.place  district=academy  fn=guide
The library is through the main hall on the left. The workshop's behind it, with the Academy workbench, if you need to make something.
The training posts behind the building are free after lessons. Mind the third one; it leans.
The Hokage Residence is just over there, the round building with the fire sign on its roof. Don't mix them up; plenty do.
If you're looking for an instructor after hours, try the staff room window. There's always a lantern lit over the marking.
The Umino houses on the streets around here belong to the teachers' family. Knock if you're lost; somebody will be marking.

## chat.place  district=academy  fn=worldbuild,guide
The Academy kitchen sells rice balls to pupils at two ryo. They're small, but they're the cheapest lunch in the village.
Exam week, the whole street goes quiet. Even the dango stall turns its brazier down so the pupils can think.
The yard's back fence has a gap the size of an eleven-year-old. Officially, nobody knows about it.

# -- the market ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## chat.place  district=market  fn=worldbuild
The market street wakes at seven when the shutters go up, and by eight you can't hear yourself think for the haggling.
Market Day is once a week. The farmers come in from outside the wall, and the whole street smells of fresh radish and grilled squid.
The two tea houses on the corner have been undercutting each other for a month. The regulars are thrilled; the owners aren't.
Every stall has a cat. Nobody knows whose cats they are, and they all seem to know exactly when the fish stall restocks.
The Akimichi compound is right off the market street. You can smell their kitchen from the fishmonger's.
Lanterns go up over the street every evening. By dusk the market looks like a festival even on a Second Day.

## chat.place  district=market  fn=guide
The grocer is halfway down on the left, under the green awning. Onigiri are three ryo, and the shop takes part-time help in the mornings.
The weapon shop and forge are at the far end, past the scroll shop. Follow the sound of hammering.
The barbecue house is round the corner by the Akimichi gate. Look for the smoke and the queue.
The pawn shop is in the alley behind the tea houses. It buys what bandits drop, and it never asks where you found it.
The furniture shop's showroom is at the top of the street, with everything it sells laid out as display pieces.

## chat.place  district=market  fn=worldbuild,commerce
Prices on the market street climb three days before a festival. Buy your lanterns early, or pay double.
The fish stall sells sardines for a ryo each. Cheapest protein in the Leaf, if you can stand the smell on your hands.
The haggling rule here is: first price is a joke, second price is an insult, third price is the price.

# -- the main street -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## chat.place  district=main_street  fn=worldbuild
The main street runs from the gate straight to the Hokage Residence. Stand in the middle and the faces on the Rock look right at you.
Ichiraku's lanterns are the first thing you see coming in from the gate. Half of the Leaf's homecomings end on those stools.
The Yamanaka flower shop is on the main street, so the whole street smells of lilies by noon in summer.
Processions use this street: the police parade, the Leaf Festival, the lantern walk. The cobbles have heard a lot of drums.
The utility poles along the main street are the only ones in the village with paper lanterns. Genin use them for jumping practice. They're not supposed to.
Shop signs up and down the street are painted by the same family on the artists' lane. You can tell by the curl on every last stroke.

## chat.place  district=main_street  fn=guide
Ichiraku's on the left before the crossroads. A bowl's six ryo, and they take on help at the counter.
The Hokage Residence is at the end of the street, under the Rock. The Mission Desk is on its ground floor.
The main gate is the other way, back down the street. Big doors, painted characters, guards who'll ask your business.
The tailor is by the crossroads, three doors along. Outfit variants start at a hundred ryo; mending is cheaper.
The bath house is off the main street down the lane with the blue curtain. Look for the steam.

## chat.place  district=main_street  fn=worldbuild,guide
Every new arrival in the Leaf walks this street first. Every squad home from a mission walks it last.
Market Day overflows from the market street onto this one, and the cart traffic stops dead at the crossroads for an hour.
The noticeboard outside the Residence lists the week's events and lost pets. The lost pets column is always longer.

# -- residential streets -------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## chat.place  district=residential  fn=worldbuild
The residential streets are mostly civilian families. Laundry on every balcony, shoes on every step and a cat on every wall.
Every house on this street has a round water tank on the roof. On a hot day, the children climb up and dangle their feet in them.
The old grandmother at the end of the lane sits out on her step at four every afternoon. Nothing happens on this street she doesn't see.
These streets are quiet at night apart from the occasional shinobi landing on a roof. The residents just turn over in bed.
Somebody on this street is always cooking. Grilled fish at six, miso at noon, and at midnight, someone's mother frying tempura.
The street shrine on the corner has a fresh rice ball every morning. Nobody admits to leaving it.

## chat.place  district=residential  fn=guide
The Haruno family lives on the street with the cherry tree in the front garden. Their shop's on the market street, though.
The garden district past the bend is where the Yuhi grove is. The old trees are lovely, but don't wander in uninvited.
Cut through the alley by the bathhouse and you'll come out on the main street. It saves a good five minutes.
The Aburame house is at the wooded edge of the district. Walk slowly past their garden and don't swat anything.

## chat.place  district=residential  fn=worldbuild,guide
Children play kick-the-can in these lanes until the dinner bells. If one comes flying your way, kick it back.
The house plaques by every door show who lives there. Newcomers' plaques are always the shiniest.
The neighbourhood bath on Third Lane is cheaper than the big one off the main street. Smaller tubs, louder gossip.
Moving day on this street is a festival of its own. The whole lane turns out to carry boxes and judge the furniture.

# -- the gate ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## chat.place  district=gate  fn=worldbuild
The main gate's doors are each painted with a single character. Together they spell out what the Leaf is for, if you think about it.
The guard booth by the gate has two chairs, one kettle and a logbook that's filled every month.
Carts come through at dawn, squads go out after eight, and travellers trickle in all afternoon. You could tell the time by the gate.
The Inuzuka compound is just inside the wall here. You'll hear the kennels long before you see them.
The gate barracks house the guard and the Shiranui family, whose wall of senbon targets is something to see.
Squads coming home stop under the gate and look up at the Rock. Every one of them, every time. I don't think they know they do it.

## chat.place  district=gate  fn=guide
Outside the gate is the outer road and the training fields. Beyond the hills, you need a mission scroll and a stamp.
The Inuzuka clinic is just inside the wall to the right. That's where to go if you want to adopt a ninken pup.
Get your mission paperwork stamped at the Mission Desk before you come here. The guards won't let a squad out without it.
The Tobari lanes are just behind the wall here, dark and quiet. Mind your voice if you cut through after sundown.

## chat.place  district=gate  fn=worldbuild,guide
The gate's busiest day is Market Day. Carts back up halfway to the far fields, and the guards get very little tea.
The wall walk is open to anyone with a reason to be up there. Ask the guards; on a clear day you can see three valleys.
The waystation on the east road is a morning's walk past the gate. Merchants rest there before the last stretch.
Travellers sign the book in the booth. The book from fifty years ago is still in the archive, if you ask nicely.

# -- the Hokage Rock -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## chat.place  district=hokage_rock  fn=worldbuild
Four faces, each one taller than a house. On a clear evening the sunset catches the left side of every nose.
The stone at the top of the Rock is forest. Nobody built up there, and the deer from somewhere seem to like it.
Children climb the stairs beside the Rock on dares. The real dare is touching the eyebrow of the second face, and nobody ever has.
Every Hokage's face was carved by hand over a whole season. The stoneworkers' scaffolds stayed up for months.
There's room on the cliff for more faces. Everyone in the village has opinions about who should go up next.
The view from the top takes in the whole village and the wall all round. It's the only place where the Leaf looks small.

## chat.place  district=hokage_rock  fn=guide
The stairs up the Rock start behind the Residence. It's a long climb, so take water, and maybe a dango.
The Shimura house is just under the Rock. It's very quiet, and they prefer it that way. Walk past it softly.
At the top, follow the path to the left for the best view of the main street. The right path goes nowhere but trees.

## chat.place  district=hokage_rock  fn=worldbuild,guide
In the mornings the Rock's shadow covers half the village. By noon it's pulled back to the Residence's doorstep.
Pigeons nest in the ears of the third face. The cleaners go up once a year, and the pigeons come back the next week.
Sit on the bench at the base and watch who walks past. Everyone in the Leaf crosses this square eventually.
New Genin climb to the top on their first free day. It's not a tradition anyone made; it just keeps happening.
The Senju compound sits at the foot of the Rock, beside the Residence. You can see their great tree from the stairs.

# -- the Hokage Residence ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## chat.place  district=residence  fn=worldbuild
The Residence is round, with a fire sign painted on the roof. From the Rock you can see it like a target.
The ground floor is all corridors and queues: the Mission Desk, the clan registry and a bench nobody ever gets to sit on.
The Hokage's office is upstairs. The window faces the village, and the lamp in it is usually lit long after dark.
Squads waiting for a mission brief trade gossip in the hall. Half the village's rumours start on that bench.
The hawk loft is on the roof. The courier birds go out at dawn and sit on the railing in a row at dusk.
The clan registry keeps every family's records. Its clerk can tell you which clan built which house, if you've got an afternoon.

## chat.place  district=residence  fn=guide
The Mission Desk is on the ground floor, open eight till six. It takes Genin and up; Academy pupils get turned away politely.
The clan registry is the door past the Mission Desk. That's where clan matters are filed and family names recorded.
The Academy is just next door. People mix the two buildings up all the time; this one is round.
Don't go upstairs unless you're called. The guards on the landing are very polite about it, twice.

## chat.place  district=residence  fn=worldbuild,guide
At eight in the morning the Mission Desk queue reaches the front steps. By ten it's mostly Chunin grumbling about D-ranks.
Notices for the week are pinned by the front door: festivals, curfews and requests for help.
The Residence garden has one plum tree, planted by the first Hokage. Nobody's allowed to pick from it, so the birds do.
The hall bench has a carved fire sign on the armrest. Rubbing it before a mission brief is supposed to bring luck.

# -- the training grounds ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## chat.place  district=training  fn=worldbuild
Training Ground Three has three stumps in a row and a memorial stone shaped like a kunai. Every squad in the Leaf has stood there.
The lake on the far grounds is for water-walking. On a calm morning you'll see Genin falling in one after another like frogs.
The climbing cliff has scuff marks halfway up and very few above. That's where most people's chakra runs out.
The groundskeeper rakes the clearings every evening. By morning there are footprints all over them, and the raking starts again.
Logs, target posts and dummies line the edges of every ground. Most of them have more kunai holes than wood.
The Gekko dojo is by the grounds, with the moon platform on its roof. On full moons you'll hear swords from there till late.

## chat.place  district=training  fn=guide
Ground Three is past the stream, through the trees. Follow the sound of shuriken.
The lake for water-walking is on the eastern grounds. Sneak to sink if you need a rest; just don't do it in the deep middle.
Sparring partners hang about the posts after noon. Ask a ranked resident for a round; it ends at thirty percent, nobody dies.
The Namiashi dojo is just past the training grounds. You'll know it by the polished floor and the very quiet people.

## chat.place  district=training  fn=worldbuild,guide
There's an unbeatable post on the far ground. Nobody's ever knocked it over, and everyone has tried.
Mornings are for squads with a sensei; afternoons are for anyone. Night is for people who can't sleep.
The water barrel by the stumps is refilled every morning. Drink from it; the groundskeeper won't mind as long as you put the ladle back.
The trees around the grounds have scuff marks on every trunk at the height of a jumping Genin. That's how you find the tree-walking route.

# -- the hospital --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## chat.place  district=hospital  fn=worldbuild
The hospital is the tallest building after the Residence. The top floor windows face the Rock, so patients wake up to the Hokage.
Anyone who falls in a fight wakes up here the next morning, patched up and rested. Nobody sends a bill.
The Suzuran herb gardens are just behind, with the drying racks along the fence. The whole ward smells faintly of mint.
Visiting hours are ten to six, but the window ledges on the second floor have sandal prints from midnight visitors.
The children's ward has painted walls: frogs, Hokage and a very wobbly deer. The painters' lane did it as a gift.
The medics take tea on the roof at three. If you hear laughing from up there, the morning went well.

## chat.place  district=hospital  fn=guide
The front desk is through the main doors. Tell them who you're visiting and they'll point you to a floor.
The hospital stall by the entrance sells soldier pills, sixty ryo each. They taste dreadful and they work.
The herb gardens are round the back. Don't pick anything; half of it's medicine and the other half is the opposite.

## chat.place  district=hospital  fn=worldbuild,guide
The night shift is always short of hands. If you ever want to help, ask at the front desk after dark.
The bench outside the front door is where families wait. Sit with them sometimes. It helps more than you'd think.
The hospital kitchen makes rice porridge for every patient whatever's wrong with them. Even a broken ankle.
The bell on the roof only rings when a big group comes in from the gate. When it rings, the whole street makes room.
Your bed here clears your fatigue like your own does, but you won't wake well rested. Hospital pillows are a mission of their own.

# -- the shrine ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## chat.place  district=shrine  fn=worldbuild
Two hundred steps up to the shrine, and the priest sweeps every one of them each morning.
The wish plaques hang by the gate in rows. Some are about exams, some are about pets, and a lot are about ramen.
The rope over the main hall is renewed every autumn. Eight people, a ladder and a lot of opinions about knots.
New Year's Eve, the queue for the bell reaches the bottom step. Everyone gets one ring, and the shinobi get told to stop showing off.
The stone fox statues by the gate wear knitted caps in winter. Somebody steals one every year.
The big cedar behind the hall is older than the village. Nobody ties fortunes to it; those go on the little pine.

## chat.place  district=shrine  fn=guide
The wish plaques are five ryo at the little hut. Write yours and hang it on the left wall; the right one's full.
Fortunes are two ryo. Bad ones get tied to the pine by the gate and left behind.
The path behind the hall leads down to the spring, where the tea houses get their water. Don't drink from the top basin; that's for washing hands.

## chat.place  district=shrine  fn=worldbuild,guide
Weddings are held here in spring. The whole stair turns into a procession and the dango sellers set up at the bottom.
The shrine bell rings at dawn and dusk. If you hear it at another hour, somebody's been dared.
Shinobi come here the night before long missions. They don't say much, just stand under the cedar for a while.
The view from the top step takes in the whole east side of the village. It's the best place to watch a festival's fireworks.

# -- the river -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## chat.place  district=river  fn=worldbuild
The river runs right through the south of the village. Anglers on the banks, laundry on the stones, children in the shallows.
The ferry crosses where the river's too wide for stepping stones. They're building a new bridge downstream, and the ferryman is not pleased.
The washing stones by the second bridge have been used so long they're worn smooth as soap.
Herons stand in the reeds at dawn like little guards. The fishermen say a heron on the left bank means a good day.
The river families mend nets on the far bank every morning. They know more about the village than the gate does.
The Namiashi children practise on the stepping stones before breakfast. You'll hear them falling in.

## chat.place  district=river  fn=guide
The ferry landing is downstream from the second bridge. A crossing is one ryo; dogs ride free if they sit.
The best fishing spot is upstream of the mill, where the water slows down. Bring a lunch; the fish take their time.
The fish stall's supplier lands the catch at the jetty below the bridge at sunrise. That's where to buy if you want it fresh.

## chat.place  district=river  fn=worldbuild,guide
Summer evenings, the riverside families have a rowing race. No jutsu allowed, and the shinobi bet on it anyway.
The lanterns floated on the river at the end of summer drift all the way to the wall grate. The Tobari fish them out by dawn.
The water's warm enough for paddling by midsummer. The deep pool under the mill is cold enough to make you yell in any season.
Eel man by the second bridge sells grilled skewers at dusk. When the sauce runs out, he closes, so go early.

# -- the apartment blocks ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## chat.place  district=apartments  fn=worldbuild
The apartment blocks near the Residence are mostly shinobi: Genin, clanless Chunin, a few old Jonin who never wanted a house.
Every landing has a shoe rack and a notice board. The board is mostly rules about noise and complaints about the noise rules.
The roof is where everyone hangs laundry, watches fireworks and practises jutsu they've been told not to practise indoors.
The landlady lives on the ground floor and hears every door. Whatever time you came in, the ground floor knows, and has opinions.
Water tanks on the roof feed every flat. In summer the morning shower is warm and the evening one is warmer.
The stairwell smells of whatever the third floor is cooking. Firedays it's curry, and nobody knows who's making it.

## chat.place  district=apartments  fn=guide
The furniture shop is a short walk up the main street. Your flat looks better with more than a futon in it.
The bath house is two streets over if your flat's tub is too small, which it is.
Ichiraku's the closest dinner to the blocks. Most of the building eats there twice a week, some of us more.

## chat.place  district=apartments  fn=worldbuild,guide
The child upstairs practises jutsu at midnight. Thumps, shouts, the occasional smell of smoke. You get used to it.
Furnish your flat however you like; the walls are yours. Just don't put a forge where the landlady can see it.
Moving into the blocks, you get a futon, a key and a stern look from the landlady. Everything else you earn.
The block's notice board lists part-time shifts around the neighbourhood. That's where most Genin find their rent.

# -- the clan quarters ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## chat.place  district=clan_quarter  fn=worldbuild
Every great clan has a gate, a main hall and a few houses behind its wall. The crest on the gate tells you whose ground you're on.
The clan quarters are quieter than the rest of the village. Fewer shops, more gardens and a lot of watching from windows.
Clan days change the whole quarter. Lanterns on the gate, food in the hall and cousins visiting from every corner of the Leaf.
Some quarters stand side by side: the Senju and the Uzumaki memorial, the three-clan alliance by the market. Others keep their distance.
Every clan's gate is guarded by somebody, but most of them are grandparents on a bench with a pot of tea.
The walls of the quarters are low enough to see over. They're for saying whose house it is, not for keeping anyone out.

## chat.place  district=clan_quarter  fn=guide
The Uchiha quarter is out at the edge of the village, walled, with the police station in the far corner.
The Hyuga estate is a short walk from the Residence. The koi pond is just inside the gate; bow before you look at it.
The Nara house backs onto their deer forest at the village edge. The deer are protected; don't chase them.
The Inuzuka compound is by the main gate. Follow the barking.

## chat.place  district=clan_quarter  fn=worldbuild,guide
Visitors bow at a clan gate before stepping through. Not deeply; just enough to say you know whose ground it is.
Clan members who make Chunin often move back behind their family's gate. Plenty stay in the apartment blocks for the noodles.
The clan registry at the Residence keeps the records of every quarter. If you want to know who lives behind a gate, start there.
Clan quarters host clan days on different weeks, so somewhere in the Leaf there's nearly always a lantern on a gate.
