# Residents teaching the beta's mechanics in their own words, one mechanic per line (ROADMAP §7).
#! fn: teach

# -- fatigue and sleep ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## chat.teach
Stay up past one in the morning and your legs go heavy and your chakra comes back slow. Tired is the body's polite warning.
Push on to three and it stops being polite. Exhausted shinobi stumble, hit soft and miss their jumps.
If the sun comes up and you still haven't slept, you'll drop where you stand and wake in your bed at noon. Ask anyone who's tried.
Get into your own bed before midnight and you'll wake Well Rested. Your chakra comes back quicker all morning.
Your own bed is the one that does the job properly. A hospital cot or a camp bedroll will clear the tiredness, but you won't wake fresh.
Sleep at night and the hours just slip by. You lie down in the dark and open your eyes at six with the sparrows.
A hot meal won't fix a sleepless night, but it does put some spring back in tired legs for an hour or so.

## chat.teach  job=medic|nurse
Half my night patients are Genin who stayed up and then wondered why their jutsu fizzled. Sleep before midnight. Doctor's orders.
A cup of green tea pushes the tiredness back an hour. It doesn't cancel it; it just moves the bill to later.
Exhausted is no state to train in. Your strikes lose a fifth of their force and the edges of everything go dark.

## chat.teach  job=tea_master
Green tea in the evening buys you another hour before the tiredness catches up. A pot at ten and you'll still be sharp at two.

## chat.teach  personality=protective|warmhearted
It's getting late. If you're out past one you'll start dragging your feet, so go home while it's still easy.

# -- ryo, the stipend and money ------------------------------------------------------------------------

## chat.teach
Ryo comes in notes: ones, tens, hundreds and the big thousand note nobody ever wants to break. Keep them in a pouch or a chest.
Academy pupils without family get a stipend of thirty ryo a day. It's not much, but it covers ramen and a little over.
Notes are just paper. Put the big ones in a chest at home and carry only what you mean to spend.
The cheapest meals in the village are three to ten ryo. A day's honest work covers that twice.
Furniture sells back at half what it cost. Think before you buy the expensive lamp.
The pawn shop buys bandit-camp salvage. That and selling furniture back are the only ways to turn things into ryo.

## chat.teach  job=merchant|pawnbroker|grocer
A thousand-ryo note is lovely to hold and terrible to spend. Change it at a big shop, never at a dango stall.

## chat.teach  player_rank=academy
The stipend stops when you graduate, so save a bit now. Genin pay their own way, mostly from missions.

# -- your apartment, furniture and chests --------------------------------------------------------------

## chat.teach
Your flat is yours to arrange. Inside your own walls you can set furniture down, turn it and pick it back up again.
Hold a piece of furniture at home and you'll see where it would go before you set it down. Turn it till it sits right.
Pick a chest back up and everything inside comes with it. No need to empty it first.
Step out of your own flat and you can't place furniture anymore. The landlady's hallway is not your living room.
Whatever else you move, a home always keeps one bed. That's the one that clears tiredness properly.
A chest at home is the safest place for ryo and spare kit. Nothing in a chest gets lost if you fall on a mission.
Potted plants, scroll shelves, lamps, cushions: it all counts as furniture, and it all makes a flat feel like a home.
A workbench in your flat means you can make smoke bombs and simple furniture without walking to the Academy.

## chat.teach  job=carpenter
Bring planks and cloth to a workbench and you can build simple furniture yourself. Cheaper than mine, though not as pretty.
A full set of furniture for a flat takes about a week of honest work to pay for. Start with a table and build from there.

## chat.teach  district=apartments
You can put a pet bed by your futon. A pet that has its own spot settles at home much faster.

# -- shops and part-time jobs --------------------------------------------------------------------------

## chat.teach
Shops open around seven. Come before that and you'll be knocking on shutters.
Part-time shifts at Ichiraku, the grocer, the flower shop or the forge pay forty to eighty ryo. Ask the owner; they're always short-handed.
A shift at a shop pays about as well as an easy D-rank. It's a good way to fill an afternoon between lessons.
Work the forge often enough and the smith will start teaching you. Some trades pay in knowledge as well as ryo.
Shopkeepers remember who works hard. Do a few good shifts and you'll get a friendlier price at the counter.

## chat.teach  job=ramen_cook|grocer|florist|smith
I'm always looking for help on busy shifts. Ask me when you've a few hours, and you'll go home with ryo in your pocket.

## chat.teach  market=yes
Market Day is when the shops need the most help. If you want a shift, today's the day to ask.

# -- food and dish buffs -------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## chat.teach
Ramen puts a little chakra back in you. That's why Genin queue at Ichiraku straight off the training ground.
Dango speed up how fast your chakra returns. A stick or two before training and you'll go longer between rests.
Barbecue puts weight in your punches. Not a lot, but enough that the taijutsu crowd swears by it.
Green tea holds the tiredness off. A cup late in the evening buys you an hour before your legs go heavy.
The good feeling from a meal doesn't last forever. Eat close to when you need it, not at breakfast for a fight at dusk.
You still need to eat ordinary food too. A buff won't fill an empty stomach.
A cooking pot at home lets you make Leaf dishes yourself. Buy the ingredients at the grocer for a fraction of a shop meal.

## chat.teach  job=cook|ramen_cook|dango_maker|barbecue_cook|tea_master
Every dish in this village does something for you. Ramen for chakra, dango for recovery, barbecue for strength, tea for late nights. Pick your meal for your day.

## chat.teach  personality=warmhearted|gentle
Eat something before you train, please. A full belly carries you further and you'll get less tired on the way home.

# -- pets ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## chat.teach
You can adopt a ninken pup at the Inuzuka clinic by the gate. Ask the vet; the pups do most of the deciding.
The shelter has cats looking for homes. They're quieter than dogs and they'll keep the mice out of your rice.
A pet follows you about and waits at home when you go somewhere it can't come. It'll be at the door when you get back.
Pets sleep at the foot of your bed. Expect a warm lump on your feet every morning.
Pets get knocked out, not killed. If yours gets hurt, it'll be back on its feet and sulking by evening.

## chat.teach  job=vet|kennel_keeper
Once you're a Genin, a ninken can follow scents on missions. A dog's nose finds what a squad's eyes miss.
A pup wants feeding, walking and a corner of its own. Give it those and it'll give you everything else.

## chat.teach  clan=inuzuka
A ninken's partner feeds it first and eats second. That's the first thing we teach anyone who takes a pup home.

# -- the Mission Desk ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## chat.teach  player_rank=genin|chunin
The Mission Desk is open eight till six on the Residence's ground floor. Come early; the good D-ranks go first.
D-rank missions pay sixty to a hundred and fifty ryo. A day of them covers food and leaves you half to save.
C-rank work pays three to six hundred. More ryo, more road and a lot more that can go wrong.
Fall on a mission and it fails: no pay, and the client remembers. Know when to pull back.
Missions give the most experience of anything you can do. A squad that takes them steadily levels up fastest.
The desk staff remember who finishes their work. Turn in a sloppy D-rank and you'll get the next one cleaning gutters.

## chat.teach  player_rank=academy
The Mission Desk opens to you once you make Genin. Until then, it's lessons, part-time shifts and odd jobs for the neighbours.
After graduation, the Residence's ground floor will be your second home. The Mission Desk hands out work from eight till six.

# -- hand signs, chakra and meditation -----------------------------------------------------------------

## chat.teach
Twelve hand signs: Rat, Ox, Tiger, Hare, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Ram, Monkey, Bird, Dog and Boar. Every jutsu is a sentence made of them.
Get one sign out of order and the jutsu fizzles. Nothing explodes; it just doesn't happen, and everyone sees.
Each sign takes a moment to form. Long jutsu are slow, so start weaving before you need it, not after.
Use a jutsu often enough and it becomes second nature. A mastered jutsu takes fewer signs and comes faster.
Your chakra comes back slowly on its own. Crouch low and keep perfectly still, and it fills much faster.
A soldier pill fills your chakra in a hurry. Sixty ryo each, and they taste like old boots.
Your chakra grows with every level. The Genin who seemed to have endless chakra just trained more.
Clone, Transformation and Substitution are the Academy's three. Learn the signs until your fingers do them in your sleep.

## chat.teach  rank=chunin|jonin
Meditating is just stillness. Sneak low, stop moving, breathe. The chakra comes back like a tide.
Mentors teach jutsu when they trust you. Train with them, prove your level, and they'll show you the next one.

## chat.teach  job=teacher
Clone is Ram, Snake, Tiger. Say it out loud as you weave until your hands stop needing your mouth.
Transformation is Dog, Boar, Ram. Practise on a friend's face first; at least they'll tell you when the nose is wrong.

# -- taijutsu: combos, dodging, blocking, substitution -------------------------------------------------

## chat.teach  rank=genin|chunin|jonin
Three quick strikes make a chain, and the third knocks your opponent back. After it, give yourself a breath before the next.
Hold a strike a moment longer and it lands as a heavy blow. Slower to come, much harder to stop.
A dodge is a quick dash in any direction. For a heartbeat, nothing can touch you. It costs a little chakra and needs a moment to recover.
Block with an open hand and you'll take less than half of what's coming. Better bruised than broken.
Substitution is a dodge timed just as the hit lands, with enough chakra behind it. You're gone, and a log takes the blow.
Substitution only works once every twenty seconds or so. Use it, then fight like you don't have it.
Watch an enemy's shoulders before they swing. A long wind-up is your cue to dodge.
Keep a chain going and your opponent never gets the breath to answer. Stop halfway and they will.

## chat.teach
Anyone can dodge. The trick is dodging toward the opening, not away from the fight.
An empty hand blocks better than a kunai. Hold it up, take the hit on the forearm and keep your feet.

# -- sparring, death and the hospital ------------------------------------------------------------------

## chat.teach
A spar ends at thirty percent health, or when someone yields. Nobody gets hurt badly, and nobody loses anything but pride.
Sparring with a ranked resident at the training grounds is a good way to make a friend. Losing well counts too.
Fall in a real fight and you wake in the hospital the next morning. No bill, and your tiredness is gone.
A bad fall costs you a level's worth of progress. You never drop below your rank's floor, and you never lose the rank itself.
Any kunai and shuriken you were carrying when you fell are gone. Keep your best kit in a chest at home.
Fight experience falls off if you keep beating the same kind of opponent. Missions and training teach more.

## chat.teach  job=medic|nurse
Everyone who falls is brought here and revived. You'll wake in one of our beds at dawn, fatigue cleared, ready to go again.
Your levels are safe above your rank's floor. A fall costs some progress, not your headband.

## chat.teach  district=hospital
Wake up here and the first thing you'll want to do is check your pouch. Thrown tools don't come back with you, I'm afraid.

# -- the workbench and the smithy ----------------------------------------------------------------------

## chat.teach
The Academy workshop has a workbench anyone can use. Smoke bombs, wire and sealing tags start there.
Paper bombs open up at the workbench once you're a Genin. Until then, it's smoke and string.
Furniture can be made at a workbench from planks and cloth. It's slower than buying, and far cheaper.
Recipes come from your rank, from mentors and from scrolls. The scroll shop sells some; the smith teaches others.

## chat.teach  job=smith
The forge at the weapon shop makes kunai, shuriken, senbon and tanto from steel. Buy the steel from me or bring salvage from bandit camps.
A blade you forge yourself can be a grade better than my shop stock. That's the reward for sweating at the anvil.
Work a few shifts at my forge and I'll teach you what I know. Recipes are earned in this shop, not bought.

## chat.teach  player_rank=genin|chunin
A small forge in your flat lets you make your own tools at home. Just warn the neighbours about the hammering.

# -- kunai, shuriken and tools -------------------------------------------------------------------------

## chat.teach
A kunai is a weapon in the hand and a throw at range. Throw it, and you can pick it back up where it lands.
Shuriken stack in a pouch and fly fast. Genin learn to throw three at once.
A paper bomb sticks to a wall or a kunai. It hurts whoever's near and never so much as scratches a stone.
Smoke bombs blind the room. Anyone who was chasing you loses track of you, if you're quick.
Wire across a path makes a trip trap. Cheap, quiet and very embarrassing for whoever finds it.
A tool pouch lets you throw from your belt without fumbling. Every Genin should have one.
Practice kunai and shuriken are blunt and cheap. Learn on those before you spend forty ryo on good steel.

# -- chakra movement -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## chat.teach  player_rank=genin|chunin
Tree walking is chakra in the soles of your feet. Too little and you slip, too much and you bounce off the bark.
Water walking is the same trick, steadier. When you want to sink and swim, just let the chakra go.
Running up a wall works for about three seconds. Pick where you're going before you start.
Charge your chakra into your legs and jump. You'll go three times as high, and you won't feel the landing while your chakra lasts.
The ninja run is faster than a sprint. Lean forward, arms back, and let your chakra carry you.
Hopping from tree to tree, or roof to roof, gives you a push forward every time. The trick is never stopping.
Every chakra movement costs a little chakra. Run out halfway across a lake and you'll get wet.

## chat.teach  player_rank=academy
Tree walking and water walking come after graduation. Until then, the trees are for climbing the ordinary way.

## chat.teach  rank=chunin|jonin
The lake on the far training grounds is where everyone learns to water-walk. The cliff beside it is for wall-running.
Out of chakra mid-jump is the worst way to learn about falling. Watch your bar before you leap.

# -- levels and growing stronger ------------------------------------------------------------------------

## chat.teach
Levels are strength; ranks are trust. Getting stronger never makes you a Chunin by itself. The exam does.
Every level gives you a bit more chakra and a little more weight behind your strikes. Every sixth level brings another heart as well.
Every rank exam asks for a minimum level. Train for it, and the exam will be waiting.
