# Small talk, part A: the answer to "How's your day?" for warmhearted residents. Their own life, in their voice.
#! tags: personality=warmhearted

## chat.personal  fn=reveal_character
I made too much miso soup again. I always cook for six, and there are three of us.
Busy! I've been knitting scarves for the gate guards. They stand out there in all weathers, poor things.
The neighbour's cat has decided my porch is home. I've started buying it fish, so that's my fault now.
I spent the morning pressing rice balls for my sister's shop. My thumbs smell of salt and seaweed.
My husband caught a cold, so I've been making ginger tea by the potful. He pretends to hate the fussing.
Good, thank you. I taught the little one next door to fold paper cranes. We have forty now and nowhere to put them.
Someone left a basket of plums on my step with no note. I've been guessing all day who to thank.
I always keep a spare umbrella by the door. Somebody always needs one, and they never bring it back. I don't mind.
I tried a new pickle recipe. The jar is either delicious or dangerous. Too early to say.
A tired day, but a nice one. I sat with the old man across the lane while he told me about his fishing years.
My mother used to say a full pot is a full house. I still cook as if the whole street might drop in.
I've been airing out the futons. Nothing beats a bed that smells of sunshine.
Lovely, actually. My daughter wrote home from her mission, three whole pages. Mostly about the food, but still!
I mended a hole in my son's flak vest and stitched a little leaf inside where nobody will see it.
The bakery gave me yesterday's sweet buns for nearly nothing. I've been handing them out down the lane.
I burnt the rice. First time in years. I'm blaming the gossip at my door, not my cooking.
I keep a list of everyone's birthdays on my kitchen wall. It's getting long. I need a bigger wall.
Today I finally learned to make a proper omelette roll. Twelve tries. The neighbours ate the eleven failures.
My grandmother's teapot cracked this morning. I glued it. It leaks a little, but I can't throw it out.
I've been sewing festival yukata for the twins down the road. Their mother works nights at the hospital.
A happy day! The stray dog I've been feeding let me pat it at last. Took three months and a lot of chicken.
I always walk the long way past the hospital. Sometimes a nurse needs a hot drink more than anyone.
I'm drying persimmons on the balcony. The crows think I'm doing it for them.
I've been teaching my husband to cook. He makes very confident, very salty soup.
The tea house lady was feeling low, so I sat and drank three cups to keep her company. I'll be up all night.
I planted beans by the fence so the children on the way to the Academy can pick a snack.
There's nothing I like more than a crowded table. Elbows everywhere, someone always spills the soy sauce.
I keep a jar of honey sweets in my apron. You never know who's having a hard day.
I gave my good shawl to someone shivering by the river. I'll knit another. I like knitting anyway.
My sister says I mother everyone. Someone has to. Half this village forgets to eat.
Quiet. I spent it cleaning out my brother's old room. He's on a long mission and I wanted it nice for him.
I think the secret to a good stew is cooking it for someone. Stew for one never tastes right.

## chat.personal  rank=civilian  fn=reveal_character
I run the noodle cart's night shift on weekends. Tired shinobi coming off duty are the best customers. Always grateful.
My stall's been slow, so I gave away the bruised peaches. Better in a child's hand than the compost.

## chat.personal  rank=!civilian  fn=reveal_character
I came back from a mission with sore feet and a bag of mountain mushrooms for the whole floor of my building.
On long missions I cook for my squad. My sensei called me the team's secret weapon. It was the dumplings.

## chat.personal  fn=worldbuild
On cold nights the families on my street take turns heating one big bath and sharing it. Saves firewood and gossip flows.
When a shinobi on my street goes on a long mission, we take turns watering their plants. Nobody has to ask.
There's an old Leaf habit: you return a borrowed dish with something in it. My cupboards are full of other people's bowls.

## chat.personal  fn=hook
The widow at the end of my lane hasn't opened her shutters in two days. I'd knock, but I don't want to intrude.
I baked a cake for the gate guards, but it's too heavy for my back to carry all that way.
My kite's stuck on the bathhouse roof again. The children made it for me, so I can't just leave it there.
I lent my good cooking pot to someone at the last festival and can't remember who. It had a dent shaped like a heart.
