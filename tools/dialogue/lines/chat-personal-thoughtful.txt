# Small talk, part A: the answer to "How's your day?" for thoughtful residents. Their own life, in their voice.
#! tags: personality=thoughtful

## chat.personal  fn=reveal_character
I've been rereading a book of travel letters. The writer complains about inns for forty pages. Strangely comforting.
Slow, in a good way. I watched the river for an hour and decided not to decide anything today.
I've been wondering why we bow lower to the Hokage Rock in the morning than in the evening. Nobody I've asked knows.
I wrote a letter to an old friend and then didn't send it. Writing it was the point, I think.
I keep a notebook of things children ask me. Today's entry: "Does the moon get tired?" I still don't have an answer.
My father used to say a question is a door you leave open. I've been leaving a lot of doors open lately.
I finished a book this morning and felt that small grief you feel when a good book ends. Do you know it?
I tried to count the stone steps up to the shrine. I lost count twice, so I've decided there are "enough".
A quiet day. I sat in the library and read the same page three times. The page was fine. My mind wasn't.
I've been thinking about how the Leaf smells different in every street. Bread here, ink there, wet dog by the gate.
Someone asked me what I'd do with a free afternoon, and I couldn't answer. So I took one to find out.
I learned that the old bridge was built without a single nail. I've walked over it for years and never once wondered.
I like to sit at the back of the tea house and guess what people are talking about. I'm almost always wrong.
My wife says I think too slowly to win an argument. I say I'm still thinking. We've been married twenty years.
I planted a tree this spring knowing I'd likely never sit in its shade. That felt right, somehow.
There's a line in an old poem: "the frog jumps; the water remembers." I've been chewing on it all week.
I spent the morning sorting my books by how they made me feel. It was a terrible system. I loved it.
I've decided patience is mostly just curiosity that's learned to sit still.
Today I watched an old man teach a child to skip stones. Neither of them was good at it. Both were very happy.
I keep meaning to learn calligraphy properly. Every brush I buy ends up holding a bookmark.
It rained on my walk and I didn't hurry. There's a kind of freedom in being already wet.
I read that the first Academy had only one classroom. I wonder what the teachers argued about.
I've been trying to remember my grandmother's voice. I can remember her laugh, but not her speaking. Odd, isn't it?
My son asked why the sky is blue. I gave him three answers and he found a flaw in each. Proud of him, honestly.
I spent an hour choosing a tea and then forgot to drink it. Typical.
I think the best conversations happen while doing something else. Shelling peas, walking, mending a net.
I've noticed the cicadas start their song at the same minute each evening. I timed it. My neighbours think I'm odd.
I've been keeping a list of words that have no good translation. "The smell after rain" is my favourite so far.
A strange thing: every time I'm sure I'm right, I learn something that makes me less sure. It's getting tiring.
I spent the evening watching the lanterns go on, one window at a time. Every light is somebody's whole life.
I lent a book to someone two winters ago. I don't want it back. I just hope they read it.

I copy old scrolls for the archive. Today's was a grocery list from sixty years ago. Eggs were cheaper. Everything was.

## chat.personal  rank=!civilian  fn=reveal_character
On watch last night I counted the stars I could name. Eleven. On a long mission you learn which ones keep you company.
My sensei told me a jutsu learned too fast is forgotten just as fast. I didn't believe that at twelve. I do now.

## chat.personal  fn=worldbuild
The Leaf's library lends scrolls for a season at a time. People say it's so you finish them. I think it's so you miss them.
Old residents still call the main street "the long road", from before the shops. Words outlive the things they name.
The shrine keeps a book where anyone may write a wish. I read the old pages sometimes. People wished for rain a great deal.
They say each Hokage's face on the Rock was carved by a different hand. You can see it in the eyebrows, if you look.

## chat.personal  fn=hook
I left my reading glasses somewhere along the river this morning. I've been squinting at everything since.
There's a word carved on the bridge railing I can't read. Old script. I'd love to know what it says.
I lost a bookmark from my late father. Plain wood, a pressed maple leaf. Probably in the library, somewhere.

## chat.personal  fn=filler

Good, I think. I'll know for certain tonight, when I look back on it.

I've been asking myself whether the river is the same river every day. The water's new. The name's old. Hm.
I watched the potter break a bowl that had a flaw only the potter could see. I keep thinking about that.
